# Architecture: Views, schedules, phases, options and graphics

Part of the architecture notes indexed in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). Keep each topic in its bullet here; AGENTS.md only lists the topics.

- **cc_views** — views are queries over the model: `generate(project, geom, view)` returns a resolution-independent `Drawing2D` (lines with `LineStyle`, fills, anchored/rotated text in paper mm). Plans (`plan.rs`), projected views (`projected.rs`: elevations, sections, axonometric, painter's algorithm ordered by pairwise depth comparison of overlapping faces; the silhouettes of a curved surface, the smooth edges to a facet turned away, are drawn only after the facets beside them, `beside`: those within a pen's width on paper, `SILHOUETTE_REACH_MM`, which painted later would cover the line, unless a face not beside them hides them first), legends, schedules (`schedule.rs`: a `ScheduleDef` in the view picks fields, sort, filter and totals; headers and some cells are i18n keys; `to_csv`), sheets (`sheet.rs`: `compose_sheet` lays out border, title block and scaled viewports in paper mm; the app paints it and `export.rs` writes it as SVG, a hand-written PDF with standard Helvetica, or PNG through resvg with text drawn as Inter outlines by `skrifa`; `view_on_paper` lays out any view the same way for `view_export`; `dxf.rs` writes a drawing as DXF R12 for `view_export`/`sheet_export`: a view at full size in millimeters (`DxfScale::model`), a sheet in paper millimeters, layers named by the caller (the element's category), dash patterns as line types, opaque fills as `SOLID` triangles, colors as the nearest ACI; `linework::clean` first makes elevations, sections and 3D views their visible linework, `view_export` `hidden_lines`: lines less what a later opaque fill covers, surface fills dropped, runs merged and chained into `POLYLINE`s by `to_dxf_clean`, the raw drawing when past its budgets) and annotations (`annotate.rs`: room/door/window tags, grids, dimensions; `projected_grids` draws in elevations and sections the grids running square to the view, as vertical lines with the bubble on top that belong to their grid, so the view's graphics hide them by category or one by one, a section showing those between the ends of its line and between its cut and its depth). Dimensions store points in the view's own drawing coordinates, so they work in any 2D view; in plans, elevations and sections each end may also reference a point of an element in its own frame (plus a height from its base or top in elevations/sections, through `View::projection`) or a level (`cc_core::dim_refs`), and every edit ends by moving referenced ends inside the same transaction (`EditCtx::finish` → `sync_dimensions`), so dimensions follow their elements and undo with them. A fill with alpha 0 is not hit-testable and only shows when its element is highlighted (room areas).
- **Schedule definitions** — `cc_core::schedule::ScheduleDef` (in `ViewKind::Schedule`): `fields`; `filters: Vec<ScheduleFilter>` (`field`, `op`: the view filters' `graphics::FilterOp`, `value`; all must pass; `MAX_FILTERS`); `sorting: Vec<SortLevel>` (`field`, `descending`, `header`, `footer`, `blank_line`; `MAX_SORT_LEVELS`); `collapse` (one row for the elements alike in every sorting level); `columns: Vec<ColumnFormat>` (`heading`, `align: ColumnAlign`, `hidden`, `total`; only formats that say something are kept, `format_of`); `appearance: ScheduleAppearance` (`grid_lines`, `outline`, `show_title`, `show_headers`, three text sizes in mm); `hide_totals`; and `bending`. The older `filter` and `sort_by`/`descending` still load: `all_filters`/`all_sorting` read them first and `normalize` moves them into the lists (the dialog does on opening). `check`/`repair` bound everything (serde defaults, no format bump). `ScheduleField::Count` is 1 per element. `cc_views::schedule::schedule_where` filters (`FilterOp::test` on the value's text and number; a value written its own way, a diameter `Ø 12.5` or a slope, is a `Value::Measured` that filters and sorts by its number in SI units, diameters in meters and slopes as rise over run), sorts by the levels' `SortKey`s, gathers rows when collapsed (`Item`: count, sums per field, the shared cell or `schedule-varies`), walks them emitting `Row::heading`s, footers (`Row::footer`: count first, totals of the totaled columns; like the totals row, a totaled first column keeps its total and the count is left out, #434) and blank lines per level (`Group`), leaves hidden columns out and fills `Table::titles`, `align` and `appearance`; `Table::header_texts` gives the headings, and `draw_table` draws the table as its appearance says (rows grow with the text; a column's alignment applies to its heading too). `cc_engine::commands::schedule`: `schedule_get`, `schedule_set` (parts replace what they name; a filter's `value` may be a JSON number, kept as text; `columns` merges by field, `appearance` is a patch); `view_set` checks a patched `def`. The app (`schedule_ui.rs`): `props` (the five *Edit…* rows of a schedule's properties, `Tab`), `open`, and `dialog` with a page per tab (`body` the fields, `filter_page`, `sorting_page`, `formatting_page`, `appearance_page`), every change through `engine_edit::view` inside the dialog's session; the table view shows the columns' headings and group footers.
- **Phases** — `cc_core::phase`: `Project::phases` (time order), `phase_filters` and `phase_graphics` are tracked as settings; each element has `phase_created` (`PhaseId(0)` = unassigned, read as the last phase) and `phase_demolished`, each view a `phase` and `phase_filter` (templates control the filter only). `Project::phase_state` gives an element's status in a view's phase (existing, demolished, new, temporary, or absent); `cc_views::graphics::PhaseView` turns it into visibility and overrides inside the `Resolver` (phase overrides rank between view filters and the category). `EditCtx::finish` gives elements and views added by an edit the creation phase (`Document::creation_phase`, the active view's) and applies `Project::phase_fixes` (hosted elements follow their hosts); `repair` resets bad references and files before version 11 get the template's phases (`ensure_phases`). Rooms are bounded by the walls standing at the end of their creation phase (`ModelGeometry::build_rooms`), and a view whose phase leaves elements out uses `ModelGeometry::build_phase` (cached per phase in `Session::view_geometry`), so later doors do not open existing walls and walls join only within the phase. IFC export writes the status relative to the last phase as the common property sets' `Status` plus the phase names (`ConstructCAD_Phasing`, `ConstructCAD_Phases`); import reads them back.
- **Design options** — `cc_core::design_option`: `Project::option_sets` (each an `OptionSet` with ordered `DesignOption`s and a `primary`) are tracked as settings; each element has `design_option` (`None` = main model), each view and template `design_options` (set → option; missing = automatic, `TemplateIncludes::design_options` off by default). `Project::shown_options(view)` gives one option per set: the view's choice, else the option being edited (`Project::active_option`, `#[serde(skip)]` session state set through `Document::set_active_option`, which moves the revision without an undo step or dirtying the file), else the primary; `option_shows` tests an element. `EditCtx::finish` → `sync_options` puts model elements added without an option into the active one, applies `Project::option_fixes` (hosted elements follow their hosts, annotations and unknown options go to the main model) and drops stale view and template choices; `repair_options` does the same on load. `ModelGeometry::build` builds the main model plus the default options (`Project::with_options`), `ModelGeometry::build_view` the main model plus a view's options in its phase, cached in `Session::view_geometry` by (phase, options); `Session::element_geometry` gives a non-shown option's element its own (element queries). The `Resolver` hides elements of options a view does not show and schedules skip them, so options never join, open or bound rooms with each other. Deleting an option deletes its elements (the primary cannot be deleted); `option_accept_primary` merges the primary into the main model and deletes the rest. IFC export writes the main model and primary options only (`DESIGN_OPTION_PSET`, `ConstructCAD_DesignOption`), import recreates sets and options by name. Commands in `cc_engine::commands::design_options`; the app's `design_options_ui.rs` holds the dialog, the Active Option menu and the property rows. Format 23.
- **Detail levels** — `View::detail` (`cc_core::DetailLevel`: `Coarse`, `Medium` by default, `Fine`; a template property, `TemplateIncludes::detail`): `cc_views::plan_of` cuts layered walls as one poché region at `Coarse` (`DetailLevel::shows_layers`), `projected::render` fills section cuts with the poché at `Coarse`, and component pieces carry `DetailLevels` (`ComponentItem::detail` → `ComponentPart::detail` → `Solid::detail`), filtered by plans, projected views and the 3D view (`SceneMesh::build_at`); commands: `view_set`/`template_set` `detail`, `component_piece_set` `detail`.
- **Visual styles** — `View::visual_style` (`cc_core::VisualStyle`: `Wireframe`, `HiddenLine`, `Shaded`, `ConsistentColors`, `Textures`; `None` reads as `View::style`, plans hidden line and other views shaded; a template property, `TemplateIncludes::visual_style`). `cc_views::projected::render` takes a `Look` (detail, style, pattern spacing): no fills and back faces' edges in the wireframe, white faces in hidden line, the same white faces greyed by the light in shaded (`ShadingSettings::grey`: the view's ambient plus the rest times the cosine to the light, as displayed, `VisualStyle::shaded_grey` at the defaults `SHADED_AMBIENT`/`SHADED_DIFFUSE`; no material colors), `Solid::appearance` and the `Solid::surface` hatches in textures (`Solid::set_appearance`); plans strip fills in the wireframe and lay `plan::style_underlay` (the faces below the cut, rendered looking down) under their linework in the colored styles. `cc_render::SceneMesh::build_styled` sets faces' colors, `SceneMesh::unlit`, `SceneMesh::grey` (shaded: white faces times that grey in the shader, cast shadows `ShadingSettings::shadow_grey`, `SHADED_SHADOW` by default; `build`/`build_plain`, for thumbnails, keep the materials' colors lit) and the vertices' `fg`/`bg` surface patterns, which `shader.wgsl` draws procedurally along the vertices' `plane` coordinates (meters on the face, see `plane_axes`; `facet_shifts` makes them run on across the smooth facets of a curved surface, for textures too) (`naga` validates it in a test). Material textures: `MaterialAppearance::texture` (`cc_core::material::MaterialTexture`: an `Arc<RasterImage>` bounded by `MAX_TEXTURE_BYTES`/`MAX_TEXTURE_PIXELS`, the repeat's `width`/`height` in meters, `offset`, `rotation_deg`; `map` turns plane meters into repeats; repair drops a bad image); `Solid::material` names the solid's material, `cc_render::Tessellation` keeps each vertex's plane coordinates (`plane_axes`: plan X/Y on horizontal faces, level across and up on the others), `cc_render::TextureSet` decodes each material's image once into a layer of a texture array (`LAYER_SIZE`, `MAX_LAYERS`, mipmaps made on the CPU; undecodable or extra images fall back to the color), `SceneMesh::build_textured` writes the vertices' `tex` (repeats, layer + 1) in the textures style and `Renderer3D::set_textures` uploads the layers (bindings 1 and 2 of the shader). Painted faces: `Element::paints` (`cc_core::paint::FacePaint`: a point on the face, its outward normal and a material; moved by `translate`/`transform`, renamed with the material, dropped by repair when the material is gone); `cc_geom::paint` finds a paint's face after each rebuild (the body face within `NORMAL_TOLERANCE_DEG` nearest the point, spread to the touching faces in its plane and the smooth facets joining it) and `ModelGeometry::build` ends by filling `Solid::painted` (face index → `Look`), which the tessellation splits into parts and `projected::render` reads per face; commands in `cc_engine::commands::paint`, the app's Paint tool in `paint_ui.rs` (3D views only, `cc_geom::paint::face_hit`). Shading: `View::shading` (`cc_core::ShadingSettings { ambient, shadow }`: the light of faces turned away, 0.3 to 1, and how dark cast shadows are, 0 none to 1 black; serde default, not written at the defaults, repair clamps it and resets it on views that take no sun; `grey`, `shadow_grey` (a shaded shadow is `1 - shadow`, never lighter than its face), `darkening` (the ground's and the unlit styles' shadows, none to black through their defaults)) is read by `Look::shading` (`Look::of`: projected faces, the plan underlay, `shadow::veil`/`ground_veil`, `shadow::textures_light`; veils go over a face's fill and under its edges and surface pattern lines) and by `Renderer3D::set_shading` (`Uniforms::shading`: ambient, diffuse, shadow grey, the ground's shadow opacity; `style.z` the unlit styles' shadow), not by `cc_raytracing`; `view_set`/`view_get` `shading` (a merge patch, `views::apply_shading`); the app's *Ambient light* and *Shadow intensity* rows (`sun_ui::shading_rows`, in view properties and the Sun Settings dialog).
- **View graphics** — `cc_core::graphics`: each `View` has `graphics` (category visibility and overrides, applied `FilterUse`s in order, element overrides, hidden elements) and an optional `template`; `Project::filters` (`ViewFilter`: categories plus rules on a `ScheduleField` or the level elevation) and `Project::templates` (`ViewTemplate`, with an `include` flag per property) are tracked as settings. `EditCtx::finish` → `sync_views` copies each template's included properties into the views that follow it and drops references to deleted elements, filters and templates, so propagation is part of the same undo step. `cc_views::graphics` resolves an element's graphics (`Resolver`: element override, then the first matching filter, then the category, attribute by attribute) and applies them to the generated `Drawing2D` (lines carry a `Pen`, fills know whether they are `cut`, material hatch lines are `LineStyle::Hatch`, texts carry their element); subcategories (#452): `CategoryGraphics::layers` (`LayerLines`, the cut lines of each `LayerFunction`; only for walls, `has_layer_lines`, repair drops others) — plans tag the lines of a cut layered wall (medium and fine) with `Pen::layer` (`plan::layer_functions`, `boundary_function`: a line between two layers is the core's when either is structure, else the one nearer the core or the middle; a face its outer layer's; free-end caps are not tagged), and `graphics::apply` gives a tagged line `above.or(layer).or(category)` from `Resolver::style_split` (`ViewGraphics::resolve_split`: element, filters and phase, then the category); `view_graphics_set` `layers` (merge patch by function name), `view_graphics_get` `layers` for walls; the VG dialog opens *Walls* to a row per function with a cut-lines cell. Sections cut a wall as one solid, so they show no layers; `cc_render::SceneMesh::build` takes a per-element `SolidStyle`. Temporary hide/isolate is session state (`Session::set_temporary`), not model state: `Session::drawing` applies it, sheets and exports use `printed_drawing`.
- **Drafting views** — `ViewKind::Drafting {}` (category `ViewCategory::Drafting`, `view_create` `kind: "drafting"`, 1:10 by default) is a 2D view independent of the model: `cc_views::generate` starts it from an empty drawing and adds only the elements it owns (`Element::owner_view`: text notes, dimensions, imported CAD drawings and pictures, `hosts_cad`), then applies its graphics. It has its own scale and detail level and visibility/graphics (`ViewKind::has_graphics`) but no template, phase or visual style; dimensions go in it (`View::takes_dimensions`) without references to the model. It is placed on sheets and exported like any view; `remove_view` deletes what it owns. The app: *View → Drafting View* (`UiCommand::DraftingView`) and the browser's *Drafting Views* group. View-owned 2D elements (detail lines, regions, detail components) belong in it the same way.
- **Callouts and detail views** — `cc_core::callout`: a floor plan, ceiling plan, section or elevation (`ViewKind::croppable`) may have `View::crop`, a `CropBox` (min/max in its drawing coordinates, `MIN_CROP`, `MAX_CROP`); `cc_views::generate` ends with `callout::crop`, which clips lines (Liang-Barsky) and fills (Sutherland-Hodgman) of everything the view does not own and drops texts and pictures outside it (view-owned elements stay whole; `d.underlay`, tags and marks follow). A callout lives on the view it stands for, `View::callout` (`Callout { parent, rect }`): a detail view keeps the parent's kind and its crop is the callout's rectangle (`rect` is `None`), a drafting view a callout points to keeps `rect`; `View::callout_rect`/`set_callout_rect` read and move either, so the mark and the crop are one value and deleting the view removes the mark. `EditCtx::finish` -> `sync_callouts` (`Project::detail_kind`, details of details up to `MAX_CHAIN`) makes a section's or elevation's detail take its parent's kind and a plan's detail its level; `remove_view` drops callouts of a deleted parent (the details stay, cropped); `repair_callouts` drops invalid crops and callouts whose parent is missing, itself, not croppable or of another kind, or with no rectangle. `cc_views::callout::marks` draws a view's callouts (`Project::callouts_in`): a rounded rectangle and a 12 mm tag on a leader with the number on its sheet over the sheet number (`plan::section_labels`), listed in `Drawing2D::callouts` (`CalloutHandle`, `callout_at`); plans skip detail views in section and elevation marks (`View::is_detail`). `cc_engine::commands::callout`: `callout_create` (parent, `rect`, `scale` default `detail_scale`, `name`, `drafting`), `callout_remove`, `callouts_list`; `view_set` `crop` (nullable; a drafting callout's rectangle), `view_get` `crop` and `callout`; `view_duplicate` drops the callout. No format bump (serde defaults). The app (`callout_ui.rs`): *View -> Callout* (`UiCommand::Callout`, `Tool::Callout`, options `AppState::callout`: scale or a drafting view), marks picked by the Select tool before section marks (`AppState::section_selection` holds the selected mark's view, so *Delete* deletes it and the palette shows its view), `Drag::Callout` moves the mark or a corner through `engine_edit::view`, a double-click opens the view, and `view_rows` adds the callout and crop rows to view properties.
- **Interior elevations** — `cc_core::interior` (issue #477, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-interior-elevations-design.md`, stage 1: four directions): `ElementKind::InteriorMarker(InteriorMarker { position, room: Option<ElementId>, rotation_deg })` (category `InteriorMarker`, a `TypeData::InteriorMarker {}` type made with the first marker) stands in a plan in a space closed by walls, with or without a room; `ViewKind::InteriorElevation { marker, side: Side, number, origin, look, auto_extents }` (browser category *Elevations*, croppable, phased, takes sun and rebar) is the wall on `side` (north, east, south, west, turned by the marker's `rotation_deg`) seen from the marker. `origin` and `look` are copied from the marker by `EditCtx::sync_interior_views` so `View::projection` and `cc_views::view_frame` need no geometry; removing a marker removes its views (`interior_views_of`), removing a room clears its markers' `room` (`markers_in_room`); `Project::repair_interior` drops views of missing markers (and their viewports), re-copies origin and look and clears dangling rooms; format 36. `cc_views::interior::resolve` works out each drawing from the space around the marker (`ModelGeometry::room_at` at the marker on its level, in the view's phase): a section frame (`projected::section_in`) from the marker along `look`, `crop_x` between the boundary's extents across, `far` up to the farthest boundary point plus the level's thickest wall; with `auto_extents` the crop runs from the level up to the lowest downward face of a ceiling, floor or roof over the marker (`covered_at`, at least `MIN_HEADROOM` up), else the room's height, else the level above, else 3 m; off, the view's own `crop`. No closed space: an empty drawing. A detail view of one keeps its own crop (`detail_kind` sets `auto_extents` off). Floor plans of the marker's level draw it (`interior::marks`): a circle and a filled arrow per view with its number (the drawing number once on a sheet), all belonging to the marker. Views are named after the room and numbered clockwise from north (`next_number` reuses freed numbers), 1:50. Commands (`cc_engine::commands::interior`): `interior_elevations_create { room | position + level, sides, rotation }` (refused where no walls close a space), `interior_elevation_sides_set { marker, sides }`; `element_get` of a marker lists its views in `derived.views`. The app (`interior_ui.rs`, stage 2): *View → Interior Elevation* (`UiCommand::InteriorElevation`, `Tool::InteriorElevation`, floor plans only: the space under the pointer previewed with four arrows, a click runs `interior_elevations_create` at that point and selects the marker), *Interior Elevations* for selected rooms (`UiCommand::RoomInteriorElevations`, in the rooms' contextual group and right-click menu), the marker's properties (a checkbox per side through `interior_elevation_sides_set`, the last one disabled; *Open* per view; rotation through `element_set`; its room), the view's *Automatic extents* (turned off, `view_set` keeps the resolved crop as the view's own), and a double click on an arrow (`Drawing2D::interior_arrows`, `interior_arrow_at`, checked first in `tools::select`) opening its view.
- **Browser organization** — `cc_core::browser`: `Project::browser` (`BrowserOrganization`: named `BrowserScheme`s with their `group_by` criteria, outermost first, and the `active` one; tracked as settings, `EditCtx::browser_mut`; serde default, no format bump; `repair` fixes ids, names, criteria and a missing scheme in use) says how the project browser groups the views. `GroupBy` is `ViewType`, `Level`, `Discipline` (`Discipline::of`: architecture, structure or a plan's `MepDiscipline`) or `Phase`; with no scheme in use the grouping is `DEFAULT_GROUPING` (view type alone). `browser::tree` builds the `Node`s (groups with a `GroupKey`, views): at each depth the views that have a value go into a group per value in its order (types as `ViewCategory::ALL`, levels and phases as the project lists them), the others go on to the next criterion beside those groups, and every view type shows at the root, empty or not; views are sorted by their level's elevation, then by `browser::label`. `cc_engine::commands::browser` holds `browser_schemes_list`, `browser_scheme_create`, `_set`, `_delete`, `_use` and `browser_tree` (`refs::browser_scheme`). The app's `browser.rs` draws `Project::browser_tree` recursively (`nodes`, `tree_row` with a guide line per depth; the open state is kept per path of group keys, and a view type at the root keeps its "+" button), and `browser_org_dialog.rs` is the dialog (*Manage → Project Browser → Browser Organization*, `UiCommand::BrowserOrganization`, and the groups' context menu): the marked scheme is the one in use.
- **Viewports on sheets** — a view is on one sheet at most (`cc_engine::commands::views::sheet_of`); `sheet_place_view` refuses a view already on a sheet, naming that sheet (`msg-view-already-placed` with `$sheet`, "A102 - Name"), unless `move_from_sheet` asks to take it off the other sheet in the same undo step. The app's browser menu offers *Place on active sheet* for an unplaced view and *Move to active sheet* (`browser-move-to-sheet`, its tooltip naming the other sheet) for one on another sheet; dragging onto a sheet and the sheet's *Add view* list never move one. 3D views are placed like any other, titled without a scale.
- **3D views of the reinforcement in 2D** — on sheets and in exports, `generate` draws a `ViewKind::ThreeD` with `rebar` as the view's hidden-line `axonometric` faded (`lib::fade`, as services plans fade the architecture) with `projected::rebar` over it (`over_faint_model`), as the 3D view shows the bars over faint solids (the analytical view keeps drawing only its members); `projected::rebar` (at the view's scale) draws a leg of an open bar seen end-on (a vertical bar from above, a starter bar's leg above its foot) as a dot through `rebar_view::dot`/`dot_radius`, as plans and sections do (at least `MIN_DOT` on paper).
