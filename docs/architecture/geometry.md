# Architecture: Derived geometry and the 3D renderer

Part of the architecture notes indexed in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). Keep each topic in its bullet here; AGENTS.md only lists the topics.

- **cc_geom** — derived geometry, rebuilt from scratch by `ModelGeometry::build` whenever the document revision changes. Walls attached to a roof, ceiling or floor (`Wall::attach_top`) follow it: `attach_wall_tops` (after the slabs) gives a straight wall's `WallGeom` a `top_profile` along its centerline, from the planes of the target's faces turned down (reaching half the wall past them, breaking where the centerline crosses their edges or two planes cross), splits its columns there and rebuilds its solid (IFC export cuts the body by the planes of those segments, see *cc_ifc*); `EditCtx::finish` → `sync_attachments` drops references to deleted targets. Walls: joins are resolved per level, among the walls that overlap in height, on the body centerline (`Project::wall_axis`, which applies the wall's `LocationLine` and the type's core layers); an arc wall (`Wall::bulge`, see `cc_core::arc`) keeps a concentric centerline, its `WallGeom::arc` maps wall-local `(u, v)` (along the arc, to its left) to the plan, faces meet neighbors at the true crossings of their lines and circles, plans draw arcs as short chords and solids mark their curved facets `Face::smooth` (no seams, silhouettes only); base/top come from `Project::wall_base_z/wall_top_z` (top may be attached to a level). Walls meet where their drawn location lines end (`WallLine::drawn`). At a T, a layered wall's structure core (`WallGeom::core`) runs on through the outer layers of the wall it meets to that wall's core (`core_through` → `WallGeom::core_ends`, the host's `layer_gaps`), and plans draw the cleanup (`layer_span`, `layer_pieces`, `boundary_segments`; no line between cores of one material); bodies, volumes and 3D views are unchanged. `WallGeom` holds columns between openings; per-wall volume is analytic (the body solid omits caps at joins). `opening_parts` gives the pieces of a door or window (component pieces, or the boxes of `hosted_parts` for old fixed types) shared by the 3D view, plans and IFC. Pitched roofs come from the weighted straight skeleton in `skeleton.rs` (a wavefront simulation with edge and split events: sloped eaves move in at `1 / slope`, gables stay; `Roof::edge_slopes` overrides the slope or makes a gable per outline edge; `Roof::edge_overhangs` (serde default, repaired like the slopes, set by `roof_edge_set`'s `overhang`, kept by mirrors since roofs keep their point order, cleared with the slopes by `sketch_set` when the point count changes) gives an edge its own overhang: `roofs::outline` grows the footprint per edge (mitered; a gable step between collinear edges of different overhangs; exactly `polygon::offset` when no edge has one) and is the slab outline everywhere (build, plan view, `plan_area`, IFC flat and shed roofs); the planes stay where the roof's `overhang` puts them (`base_offset` is the eave tip at that overhang, so each sloped plane passes its wall line at `base_offset + slope × overhang`), an edge's eave sits `slope × (overhang − own)` above the base, and `roofs::eave_starts` turns that into start heights over the lowest eave: `skeleton_with_starts` holds such an edge still like a gable until the wavefront reaches its height (a start event; `Skeleton::late_eaves` for its eave strip, `Plane::start` in its heights), shed roofs keep their plane with `roofs::shed_low`, and the fallback moves its eaves with `roofs::raise_eaves`; faces per plane, hips, valleys and ridges; eaves on one line rising alike (and starting alike) are one plane, so a plane carried on past a notch or round an opening is one face with no line where it meets itself again (a face round an opening keeps it, `Skeleton::face_holes`, and the slab triangulates that face); a convex corner between two gables on a sloped eave's line, where a notch cut into the eave runs up to the corner, starts that eave's plane as a piece of eave of no length would (`Sim::seed_corners`), so the ridge runs on past the notch; with an overhang, `roofs::outline` grows such a corner (`notch_corners`) as if that piece of eave were there, moved out by the eave's own overhang: it cuts the miter along the eave's moved line, two points where the moved gable lines cross it with a piece of the eave between (one point when they meet on it), and where they cross the other way round (the eave reaching further out than the gables' miter) it keeps the miter and hands the skeleton a `CornerSeed` (`RoofOutline::seeds`, `skeleton_with_seeds`): the seed's two vertices stand still (`Sim::holds`) until the eave's wavefront reaches the corner, then that plane's edge grows from it; bounded, errors as values, the old lowest-plane construction as fallback), over outlines with openings (`Roof::holes`); `roofs.rs` builds the slab: `roofs::body` gives a roof's `RoofBody` (prism, shed plane, skeleton or the fallback's planes, with its vertical thickness), which `build_slabs` adds to the solid (`add_to`, `lines` for `roof_ridges`) and roof joins read as pieces of planes (`pieces`); roofs in a built join are built from the pieces `cc_geom::roof_join::resolve` leaves them (`roof_join::add_pieces`) at the start of `build_slabs`, and `attach_wall_tops` follows the undersides of every roof joined with the target (see *Roof joins* in elements.md). `rooms.rs` finds a room boundary by tracing the face of the wall-footprint arrangement around the room point. Beams (and strip footings) resolve their ends in `beam.rs`: an end inside a structural column stops at its face, other ends reuse the wall joins (`resolve_join`) among beams at a common depth; a beam (`Beam::bulge`, level only: repair and the commands drop the slope of an arc beam; `Project::beam_axis` moves a justified arc onto a concentric one) and a strip footing (`FoundationShape::Strip::bulge`) may bend, the bulge passed into their `WallLine`: `BeamGeom::arc` maps local `(u, v)` like `WallGeom::arc` (`dir_at`, `locate`, `axis`, and `outline` for the chorded plan outline, `footprint` being the four corners only), an arc beam's end frames into a column where each of its sides leaves the column on its own circle (`arc_exit`), its section is swept in chords (`add_to`: curved sides smooth) and its `volume` is exact (in `ModelGeometry::curved_volumes`), and `FoundationGeom::arc` holds a `StripArc` (`strip.rs`: chorded outline, exact area and volume, smooth facets); `section.rs` turns column and beam sections (a type's structural profile, see *Structural profiles*, or its legacy fixed section) into non-overlapping parts and outlines; `analytical.rs` builds the analytical model (members between shared nodes) from columns and `ModelGeometry::beams` (a beam end running over a column ends on its axis, and a column end at a beam that passes it ends on the beam's line and is no support, so a truss's cut posts and run-on chords still meet at the panel points; an arc beam's line keeps its arc (`Line::curve`, refit through the ends by `fit_arc` when they move), ends landing on it and nodes splitting it lie on the arc, and each of its `Member`s carries the `bulge` of its own part of the arc: `AnalyticalModel::arc`, `length` (along the arc) and `points` (chorded, which the analytical 3D views of cc_views and cc_render draw), and `split_arcs(n)` replaces each curved member by `n` straight ones between new nodes on the arc); `rebar.rs` lays out the bars of each `Rebar` set (an element hosted by a beam or column, removed with it) in its host: `RebarLayout::Longitudinal` bars (in a beam, those that do not fit side by side at `clear_distance` go in further `layers`), `Sides` (side bars on both side faces of a beam, by count or by spacing over the height) and `Stirrups`; along an arc beam the bars follow the arc as chords with their `Bar::true_length` the arc's at their own radius, and the stirrups are radial; `Rebar::splice` (`cc_core::Splice`: `max_length`, `lap`; `pieces` cuts a longer bar in the fewest equal pieces, odd bars half a piece off) lap splices the bars along a beam; `Rebar::anchorage` runs them on into the columns and pile caps the beam's ends frame into (`Supports` from `build::support_input`, the supports `build_beams` frames beams into; `anchor_end` along the bar's own line or circle up to the far face less the cover, at most the anchorage, a vertical hook leg where short, half a lap past the middle of a support between beams in line; `BeamRun` the developed bar spliced after; see *Anchorage of beam bars* in reinforcement.md); a column's bars stand around its section through `cc_geom::faces` (`SectionFace`s `inset` inside it, counter-clockwise: `sector_faces` exact arcs and radial sides of an annular sector, `outline_faces` the sides of any outline between its corners, a circle one closed face; `face_bars` puts a bar at every corner and those of `Rebar::faces` (`FaceBars`: a count with the two corners, or a spacing) between them, at even angles on an arc, or spreads a count; `face_loop` is a tie's center line with its true length; `across` finds where a cross-tie lands: straight across, or square to the bisector of two leaning faces): a plain box or round section with no `faces` keeps the older `perimeter`/`circle` layout, `Stirrups` follow the outline, `RebarLayout::Ties` are hooked straight bars from each bar between corners of the host's first longitudinal set to the bar opposite (none where the line would leave the cage), and a column's `splice` cuts every vertical bar in the same lifts, every other bar around the section `Splice::stagger` higher (`Rebar::column_pieces`, `around_parity`) (`rebar::column_faces` gives a type's faces to the commands); a wall's sets (`RebarLayout::WallHorizontal { side: WallSide, zones: Vec<SpacingZone>, inner }`, `WallVertical { side, spacing, offset, from, to, bottom, top: BarEnd, inner }` and `WallTies { every_horizontal, every_vertical }`, so `RebarLayout` is `Clone`, not `Copy`; `is_valid`, `in_wall`; repair keeps wall layouts in walls and the others in beams and columns) are laid out by `cc_geom::wall_rebar` from the `WallGeom`: bars `cover` inside the faces of the wall's core (`inner` sets a bar of the other family further in), horizontal bars at `levels` over each stretch solid at their height (`solid_runs` from the wall's columns, so they stop around openings; no cover at an end joined to another wall), following an arc wall with their true length, vertical bars at `stations` along the centerline over the solid heights with bent ends (`BarEnd`: a leg `across` the wall, then one `along` the bar) on the bar's own ends only, both spliced by `Rebar::splice`, and ties across the wall at every n-th level and station of the exterior face's sets; a pile cap's and a pile's sets are laid out by `cc_geom::cap_rebar` (by family role for whatever layout the cap's type has: bands between piles, bars along a line of piles, a mesh, side loops and stirrups of a beam-like cap; `Region` clips lines to the outline less the inset, exactly on a disk or a ring) and `cc_geom::pile_rebar` (bars around the section up into the cap, ties, and a spiral: one bar whose `Bar::helix` is its coil, `true_length` exact; see *Pile caps and piles* in reinforcement.md), `build_rebar` taking `ModelGeometry::piles`; and it places shaped bars (`ElementKind::ShapedRebar`: center lines in plan x/y and z above the level, no host, the type's diameter, imported property sets; what IFC import makes of an `IfcReinforcingBar` drawn as swept disks, typed by diameter and grade, and export writes back) where they are drawn, both in `ModelGeometry::rebar` for schedules, 3D and export.    
- **cc_render** — offscreen wgpu renderer for 3D views (`Renderer3D`); `render_to_rgba` reads a frame back (home screen thumbnails). Edge lines are the solids' outlines (`Solid::feature_edges`) plus the edges between the smooth facets of curved surfaces (`Solid::smooth_edges`, each once, as `Vertex::seam`: the other facet's normal in `fg`, the edge's middle in `bg`, a mark in `tex.w`), which the edge shader (`vs_edge`, with the viewer's place in `Uniforms::eye`) draws only as silhouettes: where one facet is turned to the viewer and the other away. Models far from the project origin (survey coordinates) are drawn in a local frame: `Tessellation::origin` (`local_origin`, the point of a `LOCAL_ORIGIN_GRID` (1024 m) grid nearest the model's center, `z` 0, so it stays put while the model is edited) is taken from every position, edge and surface pattern coordinate (`Vertex::plane`) before they become `f32` (image texture coordinates already drop whole repeats per face); every `SceneMesh` and `Highlight` carries it (`analytical_highlight` and `rebar_highlight` take the tessellation), `Renderer3D` moves the camera into that frame (and skips a highlight of another origin), and `elements_in_rect` and the app's `modify3d::Frame` project around the camera target. `pick_ray` builds the pointer ray in `f64` from the camera's axes instead of inverting the `f32` matrix.
- **Joined pieces** — a curved body a component cannot make in one piece (an oval lid that tilts) is drawn as slices; `cc_geom::join_pieces` (`solid.rs`), run by `ModelGeometry::build` over the solids of each placed component and of each door or window, lets them read as one body. Pieces of one element alike in material, color, transparency and detail levels (`Solid::alike`) that lie against each other on a whole face (the same points, within 0.01 mm, turned against each other) are joined when the body bends at one of those seams: faces of the two pieces meet along it at less than the angle of `SMOOTH_COS` without lying in one plane; every piece reached over such seams from a bent one is joined too, so the straight stretches of the body follow. Pieces that only stand side by side (boards, doors, tiles) bend nowhere and keep the lines between them. Joined pieces keep their own faces (volumes, picking, sections, IFC and paint are unchanged; a painted solid is not joined): each gets in `Solid::joined` the faces of its neighbors at the seams (the face it lies against and the faces around it), and the faces that bend at a seam become `Face::smooth`. `Solid::feature_edges`, `smooth_edges` and `vertex_normals` count the joined faces as the solid's own neighbors (edges of the joined faces themselves are never returned), so no view draws the seams, the facets shade together and silhouettes are found across them; a face lying against a joined face hides all its edges, and `Solid::inner_faces` lists those faces, which `cc_render`'s `Tessellation` does not triangulate.
