# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Reinforcement in floor plans, elevations and sections. These views drew no bars, except those of a set with a bar tag, so a reinforcement section needed a 3D view of the bars laid over a section of the concrete. With *Show Reinforcement* in a view's properties (`view_set` `rebar`, also in `view_get`), every bar within the view's range is drawn over the model: where the cut plane crosses a bar, a dot as wide as the bar (the ring bars of a footing in its section, a column's bars in a plan, the legs of its stirrups in a section); the parts of bars between the cut and the view's depth as thin lines (a plan down to the level below, a section up to its depth and within its line's ends), and a bar seen end-on there as a dot; nothing in front of the cut. In a 3D view `rebar` is the view's own reinforcement switch. Files without it draw as before.

### Fixed

- A bar tag in a section drew every bar of the set it tags, also those beyond the section's depth and its line's ends (the bars of a whole arc wall beside a section of it). The set's bars are now drawn within the view's range, as *Show Reinforcement* draws them, cut bars as dots.
- 3D views of the reinforcement placed on sheets, or exported, showed only the bars: the 3D view shows them over the model drawn faint, but its 2D form left the model out, so the bars of a footing stood alone on a sheet. They are now drawn over the model's hidden-line view faded, and a bar seen end-on (the vertical bars of a column seen from above, the leg of a starter bar above its foot) is a dot as wide as the bar, at least 0.35 mm on paper, instead of nothing, as plans and sections draw it.
- Cross-ties showed as straight bars in bending schedules (#438). The ties of a column (`rebar_create` `ties`) and of a wall (`wall_rebar_create` `ties`) counted their two hooks in their length but were read as one straight leg as long as the whole bar, so a 20 cm tie of 5 mm bar was written `straight 32`. A tie now carries its hooks as a stirrup does: its shape is the bar between the bars it holds with a hook leg at each end, bent by the hook's angle, both to one side in a column (a C) and to opposite sides in a wall (an S). The bending schedule writes it `tie 20 hook 135° 5 hook 180° 5` and sketches it with its hooks, bar marks tell C and S ties and different hooks apart, and lengths and weights are unchanged. Since a tie's shape changes, a project whose bar marks were frozen (`bar_marks_update`) gives its ties a new mark the next time marks are updated, and the old mark, matching no bar any more, stays listed until `bar_marks_update` `drop_unused`; marks already issued on drawings should be checked.

## [0.14.0] - 2026-11-22

### Added

- Create Similar (#405). With an element selected, *Create Similar* (CS, also on the Modify tab beside *Match Type Properties*) starts the tool that draws it, with its type: walls, doors, windows, floors, roofs, columns, beams, footings and foundation slabs, pipes, dimensions (in their mode), text, detail lines (in their style) and the other elements drawn with a tool. Several elements work when they are all of one type.
- Locked dimensions (#447). In plans, a linear or aligned dimension whose two ends lie on elements can be locked: with the padlock beside its text when it is selected, *Locked* in its properties, or `dimension_lock`. A locked dimension keeps its value: moving the element at one end moves the element at the other with it, and on along a chain of locked dimensions, in one undo step. An edit that would still change it (both ends moved apart, or the other element pinned) is refused with a message, and `dimension_set_value` gives it a new value.
- Text types take their size in Type Properties (#450). A text type has a *Text* section with its text size on paper and the end new leaders get, so a title type of 5, 7 or 10 mm is a duplicate of the 2.5 mm or 3.5 mm type with its own size. The engine already allowed it with `type_duplicate` and `type_set` `size`.
- Wall subcategories in Visibility/Graphics (#452). *Walls* opens to a row for each layer function (structure, substrate, insulation, finish, membrane) with its own cut lines, so a wall's core can be drawn heavier than its finishes, or in another color or pattern. In plans at the medium and fine levels, the lines of a cut wall's layers take their function's lines over the category's; an element's own override, filters and phase still win. The line between two layers is the core's when either is structure, else that of the layer nearer the core, and each face is its outer layer's. `view_graphics_set` takes `layers` (such as `{"structure": {"weight": 0.7}, "finish": {"weight": 0.18}}`) and `view_graphics_get` returns them for walls; view templates carry them with the categories.
- glTF export for visualization programs such as Twinmotion. *File → Export glTF…* (also on the Insert tab) and the `gltf_export` command write what a 3D view shows as a binary glTF 2.0 file (`.glb`), in meters with Y up: one node per level and, under it, one per element named after its type and id (with its id, unique id, category, type, mark and level in `extras`), one mesh part per material, materials named after the project's with their color, finish, transparency and texture, and texture coordinates in meters on untextured faces. The view's phase, design options, detail level and hidden categories apply. Exporting again gives the same names, so a program that reimports the file can keep the materials replaced in it. Plugins can run it with a `save_path` they were given.
- Plugin API 2 (#455). Plugins written for API 1 load as before; a manifest with `api = 2` can use everything below, and the community index offers such plugins only to builds that provide it. `ccad.api` is 2.
- A Plugins ribbon tab with the plugins' own buttons. A plugin declares `[[buttons]]` in `plugin.toml`: the command, a label, an SVG icon drawn in the app's icon style and tinted with the theme, a panel and a size. A `when` condition (`selection`, `selection:one`, `selection:<category>`, `view:<kind>`) turns a button off, with the reason in its tooltip. The tab shows only while some plugin has buttons. Icons are read with no external images or fonts.
- Plugin commands open a form built from their parameters instead of the palette's JSON. Numbers, checkboxes, lists and texts get their own fields, required ones first and the others under *More options*; `ui` hints in the parameter schema choose richer fields: `length` (in the project's units), `element` and `elements` (*Pick* in the view, *Selection*), `point` (picked with snaps), `level`, `view`, `type` and `material` (lists from the project), `save_path` and `open_path` (*Browse…*). A *JSON* toggle edits the arguments as text, and a command's result and printed lines show in the dialog. A command with no parameters runs at once.
- Plugin settings. `[settings.<key>]` declares a boolean, number, integer or text (with choices, limits and `ui = "length"`); the Plugins window shows a *Settings* section with *Save* and *Reset to defaults*, `ccad plugins settings <folder> <id> [key=value …]` reads and changes them, and `ccad.settings` gives the plugin their values. They are kept in the plugins folder, in `.settings/<id>.json`.
- Plugin translations. Texts written as `%key%` in the manifest, the commands and their parameters are looked up in the plugin's `locales/<language>.toml` in the app's language, then the base language, then English.
- Plugin data on elements and on the project (`plugin_data_get`, `plugin_data_set`, `plugin_data_list`; `ccad.data.get/set/list` in plugins). Each plugin keeps any JSON value (up to 256 KiB) on an element or on the project, under its own id; it is saved in the file, copied with its element and undone like any change. A plugin reaches only its own data. Files are now format 33.
- Files picked for a plugin. A parameter with `ui = "save_path"` or `"open_path"` (and `extensions`) gives the plugin that one file for the run, to write or read with `ccad.file.write` and `ccad.file.read`, or to pass to an export command, without the `files` permission. What a plugin writes reaches the disk only when its command succeeds. `[[exporters]]` and `[[importers]]` put a plugin's command in the Export and Import groups, asking for the file first.
- Plugin events. `ccad.on` runs a plugin's function when a project is opened, before and after it is saved, and after each command that changes the model (`model_changed`, with the ids of the elements added, changed and removed). Handlers may query the model and keep the plugin's own data, which joins the undo step of the change that fired them. A failing handler never fails the command, and one that fails three times in a row is turned off.
- External programs for plugins. A plugin asking for the new `tools` permission declares `[[tools]]` (such as a simulator), and the user approves it and picks the program in the Plugins window (*Browse…*, a suggestion found on the PATH, *Test*) or with `ccad plugins tools` and `--tool <plugin>.<tool>=<path>`. `ccad.process.run` runs the program without a shell in a temporary folder of its own, with a minimal environment, the input files the plugin gives, a timeout and size limits, and returns its exit code, output and the files asked for. The app stays responsive meanwhile: a progress dialog shows the program's last line of output and *Cancel*, which stops it and undoes the command; other edits wait. The command line, scripts and MCP wait for the program.
- Tools for plugin authors. `ccad plugins new <folder> <id>` makes a plugin to start from (manifest, a command, translations, an icon, a test and LuaLS settings). `ccad plugins test <folder>` runs a plugin's `tests/*_test.lua` in fresh projects with `ccad.test` helpers (`eq`, `near`, `fails`, `ok`). `docs/lua/ccad.d.lua` describes the whole `ccad` API for editors with the Lua language server. The Plugins window has *Reload*; a *Plugin developer mode* in Preferences reloads plugins when their files change and loads plugins from a development folder; a *Plugin Console* lists what plugins print, their load errors and their handlers' errors.
- Direct download links. Each release also carries its installers under names without the version (`constructcraft-x86_64.msi`, `constructcraft-aarch64-apple-darwin.dmg`, `constructcraft-x86_64-apple-darwin.dmg`, `constructcraft-x86_64.AppImage`, `constructcraft-aarch64.AppImage`, `constructcraft_amd64.deb`, `constructcraft_arm64.deb`), so `releases/latest/download/<name>` always gives the newest build. The README opens with a *Download* table of these links per system.
- Bar diameters with a decimal point (#437). *Manage → Project Units* has *Bar diameters with a decimal point*, and `units_set` the `diameter_point` field: bar tags and schedules then write `Ø12.5` and `Ø 6.3 mm` while every other number keeps the project's decimal symbol.
- Roof joins (#381). A cross wing or bay can be joined to the roof it meets (`roof_join`, `roof_unjoin`; `element_set` `join_to`): its end runs on into the other roof, or is cut, until it meets that roof's surface, forming valleys, and the other roof is cut away under it. A wing standing a little off the main roof reaches over the gap; a hip wing gets a gable at its joined end; a wing lower than the main roof meets it higher up the slope, one higher runs on level until the main roof rises to its eaves. Chains (a bay on a wing on a main roof) and several wings on one roof work. The roofs stay joined when either changes, walls attached to either follow both, and areas, tiles, framing, schedules (*Area*, *Tiles*) and IFC count each part once. `element_get` gives `derived.join` (the valleys and their length), and `roof_check` says when a join does nothing because the roofs do not meet. Deleting the main roof frees its wings. IFC export writes joined roofs as their bodies with `ConstructCAD_RoofJoin`, and import keeps those bodies as they are. Files are now format 34.
- A roof overhang per edge (#382). `roof_edge_set` takes an `overhang` (meters, `null` for the roof's own), so the eaves can reach 0.762 m out, a rake 0.3 m and an edge that meets a wall or another roof none. The roof planes stay where the roof's overhang puts them: an eave that reaches further out ends lower on its plane, one that reaches less ends higher. The *Edge Slopes* section of the properties palette has an *Overhang* row under each edge. `roof_edge_set` now leaves the slope alone when only the overhang is given.
- Pipe fittings from the series' catalog (the second part of #383). Where pipes of one series meet, the fitting is matched to the ones sold for that series: elbows by the nearest catalog angle, tees and wyes (a branch at 45°, now told apart from tees) by their run and branch sizes, couplings and reducers by their sizes. A reducing tee that is not sold is a tee plus reducers on the branch, and a reducer that skips sizes a chain of reducers; a fitting the series does not have is flagged with the reason. `mep_fittings` names each fitting ("Elbow 90° PVC soldável 25 mm") and counts the missing ones, matched fittings take the catalog's dimensions in 3D, and their laying lengths come off the pipes' cut length (`element_get` `cut_length`; pipe schedules gain `CutLength`, `Series`, `Size` and `InsideDiameter`). IFC export types each catalog fitting by its row and writes the series and size of pipe segment types (`ConstructCAD_PipeSeries`).
- The Pipe Catalog in the app (the third part of #383). *Systems → Plumbing → Pipe Catalog* (also *Manage*) lists the project's pipe series and the built-in ones by region and use, with each series' sizes and fittings; it loads series, makes pipe types of the sizes checked, imports and exports series files and deletes series, all undone by Cancel. A pipe type's properties pick its series and size and show the size's outside and inside diameters, wall and pressure class; a free type gets an inside diameter of its own. The pipe tool lists the types by series, the status bar names the fittings a new pipe makes or warns of one the series does not sell, and the *Systems* fittings table names them.
- Flip walls (#442). `wall_flip` swaps a wall's exterior and interior sides: the location line stays where it is and the wall is walked the other way, so its layers mirror about the line (a wall located by a finish face keeps that face on the line). Doors, windows and niches keep their shape and place, wall bar sets stay on the face looking the same way and at their stations, curtain grids and dimensions stay put, all in one undo step; arcs or splines too tight for the body on the other side are refused. In the app, Space flips the selected walls (and turns the rest of the selection, as `elements_turn` does), *Modify → Geometry → Flip Walls* does the same, and Space while drawing walls flips the side of the walls drawn next.
- Levels in elevations and sections can be hidden, moved and styled (#453). The view's graphics have a *Levels* category that hides them or overrides their lines (color, weight, pattern, halftone; the texts follow the color). Each view can hide one level, choose the ends that carry a head (end, start, both or none) and move a head up or down with an elbow (`level_head_set`; `view_get` lists `level_heads`). In the app, a head is dragged with Select and snaps back near its line, and a right click on a level opens a menu to hide it, choose its heads or reset it; the graphics dialog counts the hidden levels and shows them again. Heads and texts keep their size on paper, and the elevation text sits below the line instead of crossing it.

### Changed

- A view already on another sheet can be moved to the active sheet (#451). The project browser's menu offers *Move to active sheet* for it instead of *Place on active sheet*, with the sheet it is on in the tooltip, and `sheet_place_view` takes `move_from_sheet`; the move is one undo step. Placing such a view without it is still refused, and the message now names the sheet it is on.
- The metric template's bar types carry their steel grade (CA-60 for Ø 5,0 mm, CA-50 from Ø 6,3 mm up), and it has a Ø 6,3 mm type, the usual stirrup size, so imported Ø6.3 bars take it instead of a new type named after the file (#437); the imperial template gains a #7 bar to match. Both are added last, so the other template ids stay (the showcase macro's ids move up by one). Existing projects keep their types.
- Plugin commands run from the ribbon, the *Plugin Commands* menu and the palette open their form instead of the palette's JSON arguments.
- A plugin's limits are checked by the engine for every command it runs, including those run by a graph or a macro it starts, rather than only for the commands its Lua code calls.
- The example plugin `quick_rooms` uses API 2 (version 0.2.0): ribbon buttons with icons, a default height and door side in its settings, English and Portuguese texts, `ui` hints, plugin data and tests.
- Arc beams enter the analytical model along their arcs (#345). Each member of an arc beam carries the bulge, center, radius and sweep of its part of the arc, and its length is the arc's; members connecting along the arc split it at nodes on the arc. The analytical export is format 3. Analytical 3D views draw the arcs. `arc_segments` on `analytical_model` and `analytical_export` writes each arc as that many straight members for programs that take only straight members.

### Fixed

- Haunched strip footings survive their own IFC export (#435). A strip footing with a beam (an inverted T) whose ends are square was written as a faceted body and came back as a generic component, with a second type and its bars left without a host. It is now written as its section revolved about the arc's axis (an arc strip) or extruded level along it (a straight one), so importing the file gives native strips of the same type and volume, with their bar sets on them. Strips that slope or whose ends are cut at an angle by joins are still written as faceted bodies.
- IFC import gave walls, slabs, columns, beams and every other element the other program's numeric element id as their mark, as it did for bars before #369 (#436). A purely numeric `Tag` in a file another program wrote is no longer a mark; other tags, and every mark in a file ConstructCraft wrote, come in as before. Empty lines of the building's address are no longer joined into the project address as bare separators.
- A schedule whose first column is totaled showed the element count in that column of its totals row and group footers, and the column's total nowhere (#434). The total now stays in its column; the count still fills the first cell when that column is not totaled.
- *Close Other Views* from a tab bar's list of open views left the view black when its tab was not the bar's first, until the tab was clicked (#431). A bar now keeps its active tab in front when other tabs close, and when the active one closes the tab now in its place takes over.
- Walls attached to a roof, ceiling or floor were exported to IFC as a box up to their highest point, so gable ends came out as rectangles (#379). Their body is now the extrusion cut by the planes of the top it follows, and `Qto_WallBaseQuantities` takes the height and side area from that top. Import reads such a wall back as a wall with an elevation profile of its top.
- Gable roofs with a notch in a sloped eave ending at a corner of the footprint had a ridge that stopped short and a slope that ran past it, also with an overhang (#380). The ridge now runs its whole length, eaves on one line rising alike make one plane with no line from the notch to the ridge, a plane that runs round an opening keeps it open, and with an overhang the corner is cut along the eave's moved line.

## [0.13.0] - 2026-11-19

### Added

- 3D CraftHouse in the Component Library. *Insert → Component Library* has a second source beside the library folders: the open library of parametric components published at [3dcrafthouse.constructcraft.net](https://3dcrafthouse.constructcraft.net) (CC BY 4.0). Picking it reads the catalog from the site; the components are searched and filtered by kind like those of a folder, only the one picked is downloaded, and *Load into Project* brings it in with its types. *Open Site* shows the library in the browser. Nothing is downloaded until the source is picked. The window is a browser in three columns: filters by kind and by folder or author with their counts, a grid of cards with the components' 3D pictures (a double click loads one), and the details of the component picked.
- 3D thumbnails in the Component Library. Each component's card, from a folder or from 3D CraftHouse, shows a small 3D picture of its first type (a door or a window in a piece of wall). A folder's are rendered by the app the first time the component is listed and kept in its data folder. 3D CraftHouse publishes its components' pictures (the catalog's optional `thumbnail`, a PNG under `thumbnails/`), downloaded a few at a time as the cards show them; no component file is downloaded for its picture, and a component without one shows the app's own once its file has been downloaded. Pictures and components are kept, so they are there at once the next time; *Refresh* downloads them again.
- Reinforcement of pile caps and piles (#343). `pile_cap_rebar_create` reinforces pile caps in one undo step, one set per family laid out from the cap's layout and outline: bottom bars over the piles (a band between each two piles of a three- or four-pile cap, along the sides of the triangle or the square and optionally across a square's diagonals; bars along the line of a two-pile or line cap; a mesh in a single-pile, round or ring cap) lying on the piles' heads with their ends bent up the side faces, a top mesh, side loops around the outline and, in a two-pile or line cap, stirrups. `pile_rebar_create` gives piles bars around the section from a length below the head up into the cap (at most to its top less the cover) and a spiral, measured along its coil, or ties. Because the sets are laid out from the cap's geometry, changing a cap type's layout lays its caps' bars out again for the new one; the piles it keeps keep their sets, a pile it adds takes the sets of the cap's other piles, and a pile it removes takes its own along. The sets are ordinary reinforcement: deleted with their cap or pile, in rebar schedules (with their host), bar marks and the bending schedule (a spiral as `spiral R=… p=… L=…`), 3D rebar views, bar tags and IFC, which brings them back into the imported cap or pile. `rebar_copy` copies sets between caps and between piles of one type. A cap's and a pile's properties show their *Reinforcement*, and the Structure tab's *Reinforcement* group has *Pile Cap Reinforcement* and *Pile Reinforcement*.
- Anchorage of a beam's bars into the columns and pile caps it frames into (#346). Bars along a beam stopped the cover short of its ends, at the faces of its columns. With `rebar_create` `anchor` (`true` for 40 bar diameters, or a length of 10 to 100 diameters), top, bottom and side bars run on into the support at each end, along their own line or, in an arc beam, their own circle: up to the support's far face less the cover, at most the anchorage length. Where the support is too shallow, top and bottom bars end in a 90° hook (`anchor_hook`: `auto`, top bars down and bottom bars up, `down`, `up` or `none`) whose leg makes up the anchorage, at least 8 diameters and inside the support's height less the cover; hooks are drawn and count in the length, so a hooked bar is an `L` or a `U` in the bending schedule. Where two beams meet in line over a support (a continuous beam, or the arcs of a ring beam), the bars of both run half a lap past its middle and lap inside it, or reach its far face less the cover when it is not deep enough. The anchorage is added before bars are spliced; `lap_at_joins` still acts where a beam meets another without a column. `rebar_typical` takes `anchor` for the beams' top and bottom bars, `element_set` changes a set's `anchorage`, and the properties of a beam's bars have *Anchor into Supports* and *Anchorage Hook* rows. Sets without an anchorage, and files saved before, keep their bars.
- Reinforced concrete wall types in new projects (#366). The metric template has *Concrete wall 15 cm*, *20 cm* and *25 cm*, and the imperial one 6", 8" and 10", each one structure layer of the template's concrete. They are structural types, so walls drawn with them take reinforcement without further settings. Older projects are not changed. `wall_rebar_create` without `walls` now lists the walls it left out for not being structural (`not_structural`), with a `warning` when it reinforced none.
- Sweeps along arcs and circles (#366). `sweep_create` takes the path as `curves` (lines, arcs and a circle, as `floor_create` takes them) instead of points. The arcs stay arcs: the profile stands square to the path all along them, plans draw them in short chords with no lines across, the curved sides read as smooth surfaces in every view, and the volume is exact (the profile's area times the path of its centroid), so a haunch swept around a circular tank has the volume the hand calculation gives. Paths keep the arcs as bulges (`element_get`/`element_set` `bulges`), moved, rotated and mirrored with the sweep. IFC export still writes the chorded body.
- Staggered splices of a column's vertical bars (#351). A column's bars cut in lifts were all spliced at the same heights; `rebar_create` `stagger` now splices every other bar around the section higher, by half the lap (`half_lap`) or an offset in meters (at most half of `max_length - lap`). Each bar still runs from the base to the top: a staggered bar's first piece is longer by the offset and its last shorter, a splice that would come within a lap of the top is left out, and with `max_length` the lifts are made shorter where the longer first piece would not fit the stock. Staggered pieces get their own bar marks; the set's total length is unchanged. `element_set` changes a set's `splice.stagger`, and the properties palette of a column's vertical bars cut in lifts has a *Splice Stagger* row (none, half lap or an offset). Files without a stagger load as before.
- A list of the open views at the right end of each view tab bar (#371). With many views open their tabs no longer fit the bar; the *Open Views* arrow lists the bar's views by view type, as the project browser groups them, with the active one checked, and picking one brings its tab to the front and scrolls it into view. The list also offers *Close Other Views* and *Close All Views*. A view opened from the browser or a command is scrolled into view too.
- Splines in sketches (#376). The *Spline* tool draws a smooth curve through the points clicked, open or closed, in the component environment and in plan sketches. Grips sit on the points it passes through and on their handles. Masses, floors, detail lines, title block strokes and component pieces take splines (`CurveArg::Spline` with `through` or `control`, and `ProfilePoint::bezier`), and areas and volumes follow the true curve.
- Pipe series (#396, the first part of #383). A pipe type can be a size of a pipe series: a manufacturer's or a standard's table of sizes with their outside diameter, wall and inside diameter. A built-in catalog holds 22 series, Brazilian, European and American (PVC, CPVC, PP-R, PE, copper, PEX), and a series can be imported from a file or exported to one. A type of a series keeps its row's outside diameter. Sizing measures a run's speed in its inside diameter and keeps the run in its series, making the missing size's type in the same undo step. The templates' pipe types are now sizes of two series each (`BR-PVC-SOLD` and `BR-PVC-ESG-N` in the metric one, `US-PVC-SCH40` and `US-PVC-DWV` in the imperial one), so their diameters are the series' outside diameters (DN 100 is 101.6 mm). Older files link the template's pipe types by name and diameter. Commands: `pipe_catalog`, `pipe_series_list`, `pipe_series_get`, `pipe_series_import`, `pipe_series_export`, `pipe_series_delete`, `pipe_type_create`; `type_set` takes a pipe type's `series`. Files are now format 30.
- Sun shadows in 2D views and a grey *Shaded* style (#377). *Shaded* is now hidden line with grey shading by the light, plus the sun's shadows, in 2D views and in the 3D view. Plans, elevations and sections draw exact shadow polygons by drafting convention: floor plans cast from the whole model, above the cut too, but leave covered indoor spaces unshadowed; sections apply the same rule beyond the cut; elevations cast on everything. A conventional 45° sun (`view_set` `sun` `mode: "conventional"`) is the default when an elevation or section gets a sun. *Ambient light* and *Shadow intensity* are set per view (`view_set` `shading`). The curved surfaces of component pieces are smooth, so their facet edges are no longer drawn.
- A type mark row in the properties palette (#374): a model element shows its type's mark, read only, under its own mark.
- A wall type can make its walls structural (#387). Type Properties has a *Structural* checkbox. New walls made by the wall tool, `wall_create` and `mass_walls_create` take its value unless told otherwise (`wall_create` `structural`). Existing walls keep their own.
- An API reference on the site (#386), generated from the doc comments by `cargo run -p api_doc`, with an overview in English and Portuguese.
- Editable tags and view marks (#395). A tag type says what plans write by doors, windows, rooms, columns, beams, pad footings and pile caps, and how: text lines with fields, a symbol (built-in shapes or a detail component), text sizes, pens, colors, fill, leader and orientation. Each plan picks a tag type per target, and each tag can be moved, turned, given a leader or another type, or hidden in that view. Grid types set their bubble ends, symbol, text size, pen, color and line pattern, and each view moves or hides a grid's bubbles, with an elbow leader, in plans, elevations and sections. View mark types style the heads of sections and the markers of elevations (head size, symbol, texts with `{Number}`, `{Sheet}` and `{Name}`, arrow, line weight and colors), and plans move elevation markers. *Annotate → Tag Types* lists and edits these types, the properties palette has a *Tag* section, and tags, grid bubbles and markers are dragged in the view; a right click hides or resets a tag. Texts can carry a color, kept in SVG, PDF, PNG and DXF exports. A target with no tag type draws what it drew before, and the templates carry a tag type per target and a mark type per kind. Commands: `tag_type_create`, `tag_set`, `tag_get`, `view_mark_type_create`, `mark_move`, `view_set`/`view_get` `tag_types` and `mark_type`; `tag_move` keeps working. Files are now format 29.

Filters compare a field with a value (equals, contains, begins with, greater, less, has a value… the comparisons view filters have), numbers as numbers, and a row must pass every one; fields that are not shown filter too. Sorting runs in up to four levels, each ascending or descending, and a level may group its rows under a heading, over a footer with the group's count and totals, and apart by a blank line. With *One row per element* off, the elements alike in every sorting level share a row: values that differ read as `<varies>`, totaled columns add up, and the new *Count* field says how many. Each column takes its own heading and alignment, and may be hidden (still sorting and filtering) or totaled or not. The appearance (grid lines, outline, the title and header rows, the three text sizes) is how the table is drawn on sheets and exports. Commands: `schedule_get` and `schedule_set`; older schedules load unchanged, their single filter and sort field reading as the first filter and level.
- Sun studies. Plans, ceiling plans, 3D views, elevations and sections can have a sun (*View → Graphics → Sun Settings*, or the *Sun* row of a view's properties): a still moment, a study over one day or several days (every 15, 30 or 45 minutes or hour, or every day, week or month, between two times or from sunrise to sunset, the frame shown chosen in the dialog), or a light from a direction given by hand, from true north or relative to the view. The sun's position comes from the date, the time and the site location, local time following the longitude's time zone or one set by hand (`site_set` `utc_offset`). Built-in presets (the solstices and equinoxes, the whole year, two lights) and the project's own named presets set the mode in one click. The sun path diagram (a compass ring turned to true north, the day's path with its hours, sunrise, sunset and the date, and the sun) is drawn over plans, elevations, sections and 3D views, and is not picked or cropped; shaded projected views and 3D views are lit by the sun, and 3D views cast its shadows on the model and, optionally, on the plane of a level. The view cube's compass shows true north. Commands: `view_set`/`view_get` `sun`, `sun_position`, `sun_frames`, `sun_preset_save`, `sun_presets_list`, `sun_preset_rename`, `sun_preset_delete`. No format change.
- Grids in elevations and sections (#332). An elevation or a section draws the grids that run square to it, each as a vertical line where it stands with its bubble on top, as tall as what the view shows; a section shows the grids between the ends of its line that reach between its cut and its depth. ### Added

- Lua scripts and plugins (#72, first part). `ccad lua script.lua` runs a Lua 5.4 script that calls the engine's commands with `ccad.run` and keeps their results in variables, which JSON scripts cannot do (`examples/small_house.lua`). A plugin is a folder with a `plugin.toml` manifest and Lua code that registers commands with `ccad.command`. Its commands join the catalog as `<plugin>__<command>`, so `ccad run`, scripts and MCP agents call them like the built-in ones; `ccad --plugins <folder>` loads them and `ccad plugins` lists them (`examples/plugins/quick_rooms`). Each plugin command is one undo step, and a failed one leaves nothing behind. Lua runs sandboxed: no files, no loading of code, an instruction and a memory limit. Plugins query and edit the model, and they need the `files` permission to import, export or save. The new error code `script` reports a failed script or plugin. Plugins are listed in the community index, [constructcad-plugins](https://github.com/francisreboucas/constructcad-plugins); the app's plugin manager comes next.
- Plugin manager (#72). *Manage → Plugins* opens the Plugins window. *Installed* lists the plugins of the plugins folder (set in *Preferences → File locations*, by default a folder in the app's data folder), with their commands and any error; it turns them on or off, allows file access for those that ask for it, and removes them. *Available* reads the community index and installs or updates a plugin after checking the download against the index's checksum, and only if the plugin loads. *Manage → Plugin Commands* lists the installed plugins' commands and opens one in the command palette, which now also finds plugin commands. Without the app, `ccad plugins install <file.zip> <folder>` and `ccad plugins remove <id> <folder>` do the same.

- Scripted components (#170). A component piece can be a Lua script that declares parameters (length, angle, number, yes/no, text, choice, material) and hotspots, and returns pieces from `geometry(p)`: boxes, cylinders, extrusions, profiles, revolutions, sweeps, blends and meshes (`examples/components/cabinet.lua`). Parameters without `instance = true` are shared by the type; with it, each element has its own. Scripts are pure and sandboxed, run once per set of values, and a failing one keeps its last pieces and reports the error with its line. New commands: `component_script_set`, `component_params_get`, `component_params_set` (types, or several elements at once, in one undo step) and `component_hotspot_move`. The properties palette edits every parameter kind and shows the shared ones and the script's errors; the component editor adds script pieces; hotspots are dragged as grips in plans. Material parameters count as uses of their material. Projects are saved as format 18.
- Terrain. *Massing & Site → Site → Terrain* sketches a boundary and places measured points with their elevations. The points are triangulated with the boundary (constrained Delaunay through the `spade` crate), the earth closes down to a flat base, plans draw contour lines and hatch the earth above the cut, and sections cut it in the new *Earth* pattern (a woven hatch). Properties edit the base depth and each point and show the elevations, area, volume and warnings; schedules list terrain. New commands: `topography_create`, `topography_points_set` and `topography_type_create`. IFC export writes an `IfcGeographicElement` (`TERRAIN`) that imports back as terrain. Projects are saved as format 19.
- A *Massing & Site* ribbon tab holds the massing tools, which leave the *Architecture* tab, and the terrain.

Cut and fill volumes are exact between the two surfaces. Plans draw the pad with its elevation, slope ticks, the daylight line and the existing contours dashed; sections dash the existing ground. Properties set the elevation and each slope (the type's or the pad's own) and show the area, volumes and net; pad schedules total the volumes. Pads move and are deleted with their terrain. New commands: `pad_create` and `pad_type_create`. IFC export keeps them in the terrain's `ConstructCAD_Pads` property set, which import reads back. Projects are saved as format 20.

The wall tool's type list offers *+ Curtain Wall* when the project has none, *Type Properties* edit the grids, panel and mullions, and *Architecture → Build → Curtain Grid* adds and removes lines, merges and splits segments and swaps panels with clicks in plans, elevations and sections. IFC export writes an `IfcCurtainWall` with an `IfcPlate` per panel and an `IfcMember` per mullion, and the grid in a `ConstructCAD_Curtain` property set that import reads back; curtain walls from other programs are rebuilt from their parts when they make a plain straight grid.

Every engine command is a node, with ports from its parameters, beside built-in nodes (inputs, math, logic, lists, geometry, element parameters) and pure Lua nodes; a list given where a node takes one value runs it once per item (shortest, longest or cross lacing). Running a graph again updates the elements it made instead of adding copies: each keeps its id, mark, comments and phases, new ones are made and those no longer made are deleted, all in one undo step. Graphs are `.ccgraph` files; `ccad graph <file> key=value` runs one without the app, and the commands `graph_run`, `graph_check`, `graph_nodes`, `graph_elements` and `graph_release` drive them (`examples/graphs/house.ccgraph`). Projects are saved as format 21.

Sheets draw an editable title block instead of the fixed one: border, block, logos and texts drawn with the sketch tools in the title block editor (*Modify → Title Block → Edit Title Block*, *View → Documentation → New Title Block*), measured in paper millimeters and adapting to any paper size. Sheets gain portrait orientation, custom paper sizes and an issue date. Title blocks travel in `.ccomp` files and the component library. The old block is now the built-in *Standard* title block, given to older files on load with captions in the interface language. New commands `sheet_set`, `sheet_fields`, `title_block_create` and `component_sheet_piece_add`/`set`/`delete`; `project_info_set` takes `fields`. Projects are saved as format 22.
- Level marks (#202). *Annotate → Levels → Level in Plan* writes, on a circle with two filled quarters, the finished level of the floor under the point and, below the shelf, the raw level of its structure (the top of the layers under its finish); off any floor it writes the plan's level. *Level in Section* writes a point's height on a triangle pointing down to it, with the raw level when the point lies on the top of a cut floor, and snaps to the section's lines. The values follow the model; a mark can set either level by hand, hide the raw one or put the values on the left, and its type holds the text size, the datum written for the project's zero and the suffixes. New commands `level_mark_create` and `level_mark_type_create`.
- Stair step numbers (#199). *Number steps* (in the properties palette and the stair tool's options bar) writes a number in every tread and winder of the stair in plans, from a *Start number*; the floor the stair arrives at takes the next one, and landings none. `stair_create` takes `numbers` and `number_start`.
- Typed lengths in sketches (#179). After the first point of a floor, ceiling, roof, mass, terrain, shaft or component sketch, typing places the next point: a line's length and, after Tab, its angle; a rectangle's width and depth; the radius of a circle, polygon or arc; the distance of a Move or Copy. Enter places the point and Backspace edits the value.
- Drafting views (#148). *View → Drafting View* makes a 2D view independent of the model, with its own scale, detail level and visibility, for standard details: it shows only what is drawn in it (texts, dimensions, imported drawings and pictures, detail lines, regions and detail components), and goes on sheets and exports like any view. `view_create` takes `kind: "drafting"`. The scale lists of all views gain 1:1, 1:2 and 1:5.

New commands `line_styles_list`, `line_style_create`/`set`/`delete`, `filled_region_create`, `masking_region_create` and `filled_region_type_create`.

- Component pieces with parts of ellipses. A point of an extrusion, revolution, sweep or blend profile can run to the next one along an ellipse (`ellipse` in `component_piece_add`'s points: center, semi-axes, rotation and direction, all expressions).
- Component pieces are sketched with the sketch tools. *Extrusion*, *Revolution*, *Sweep* and *Blend* open the same sketch as floor and roof outlines, with its drawing tools (now also polygons, ellipses and the three kinds of arcs), picked curves with grips and values, the Modify tools and undo; points still lock to reference planes. *Edit Sketch* opens a piece's outline, path or blend outlines again, keeping the expressions of the points that stay.

### Fixed

- Assembled stairs' stringers (#142). A central stringer was a wedge growing from nothing at the foot of a flight that starts on the floor, and the stringers of flights between landings only touched the landings' edges. Stringers are now built from their side profile: a band parallel to the pitch, as deep as the type says, cut level on the floor or at the underside of the landing a flight leaves (bearing on its face), and carried on under the landing a flight rises to, up to its underside.

### Added

Plans draw the flights (dashed above the cut, with a break line), the arrow and the slope; schedules list width, slope, run and volume; IFC export writes `IfcRamp` (`STRAIGHT_RUN_RAMP`, `TWO_STRAIGHT_RUN_RAMP`, `QUARTER_TURN_RAMP`, `HALF_TURN_RAMP`, `SPIRAL_RAMP`). `stair_railings_add` (*Add railings*) also puts railings along ramps.
- Autosave and crash recovery (#51). A minute after a project gets unsaved changes (new, never-saved projects too), a copy of it is written in the background to the `recovery` folder of the app's data folder, only when the model changed since the last copy, through a temporary file and a rename. Saving, closing or quitting cleanly removes it. When ConstructCAD starts and finds copies left by a session that crashed or was killed, the *Recover Unsaved Work* dialog offers them: *Restore* opens the project with its unsaved changes (saving writes to its original file), *Discard* deletes the copy, *Cancel* keeps it for the next start.

A grid across the existing ones starts a new series: letters across numbered grids, numbers otherwise. The name is unique among the grids (a taken one is skipped). The grid tool shows the name next to the cursor before the second click. `grid_create` uses these names when `name` is left out.
- Grid ends line up with the bubbles of the other grids (#52). When the grid tool places a point or an end handle of a selected grid is dragged, the end snaps (within the usual 10-pixel snap distance) to the line through the matching end of a parallel grid, perpendicular to it. The second point also keeps the grid parallel to that grid. A dashed guide from that grid's end shows the alignment. Model points and guide crossings still take priority.
- *Align Grid Ends* (*Architecture* and *Structure* tabs, *Datum* group; `grids_align_ends`): with one grid selected, every grid parallel to it (within 1°) takes its extents. Each end moves along its own line to the reference end on its side. Pinned grids are left alone. `ends` limits the move to the `start` (bubble) or `end` ends, and `grids` picks the grids to align.

Points are picked on a horizontal working plane at the elements' level (then at the height of the first point) or snap to model points near the pointer: element corners, column and component centers at their base and top, wall ends and grid crossings. The preview shows the elements' outline where they will go. Changes are plan transforms, so elements keep their levels. Trim/Extend, Split, Offset and Allow/Disallow Join need plan linework and say so in 3D views.

Alignment is in plan, so a column of an upper floor aligns onto the face of the column below. `element_align` takes `pick` and `reference_pick` for them (without `pick`, a column or component moves its insertion point onto the reference line, such as a grid).
- Dimension lines line up with other dimensions (#54). While the line of an aligned or linear dimension is placed, it snaps (within the usual 10-pixel snap distance) onto the line of a parallel dimension in the same view, shown by a dashed guide from that dimension's line.
- Continuous dimensioning (#54). The options bar of the aligned and linear dimension tools chooses *Single* or *Continuous*. In *Continuous*, after the first dimension each click on a point adds the next dimension of the chain, from the previous point, on the same dimension line and in the same direction; Enter, Esc or a right-click ends the chain. Each segment is its own dimension, whose ends follow the elements they were placed on. The new `dimension_chain_create` command places a whole chain (points in order, `kind`, `direction` or `angle`, `offset`) in one undo step.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-24

### Added

- Arc walls: a wall's location line can be a circular arc. The wall tool gains *Line*/*Arc* modes in the options bar; in *Arc* mode click the start, the end and then a point on the arc (with a live preview, radius and length), and the chain goes on from the arc's end. Layers, faces and cut patterns follow concentric arcs; arc walls join straight and arc walls at corners, tees and junctions (faces meet where their lines and circles cross; walls continuing along a common tangent butt square), host doors and windows along the arc (offsets measured along the centerline, frames square to the wall at the opening), bound rooms and give floors and roofs drawn from walls their curve. ### Added

Beams resting on a column, or at another depth, are left whole. - Analytical model: structural columns and beams as center lines between shared nodes — beams framed into columns end on the column axis, beams ending on beams end on their line, members split where others connect, column bases are supports. *Analytical Model* opens a 3D view of it over the model drawn faint (any 3D view can switch with its *Analytical Model* property; `view_create` kind `analytical`); `analytical_model` returns it as JSON and *Export Analytical Model* (`analytical_export`) saves it for structural analysis programs.

Bars follow their host and go with it; *Structure → Reinforcement*: *Typical Reinforcement* for the selected (or all) beams and columns, a *Reinforcement View* (3D, bars over the faint model) and a *Steel Schedule* (host, diameter, bar count, bar length, total length and weight, with totals). Their types set the outside diameter and material; the template has PVC supply pipes (20–32 mm), drain pipes (40–100 mm) and flexible conduits (20–32 mm).

Each has a code shown in tags and a color. *Systems and Circuits* edits them and shows the circuit loads.

Fixtures carry drainage fixture units, a supply weight and hot water; devices are general-purpose and dedicated outlets, switches, ceiling and wall lights, and distribution boards, with their power in VA. Tags give the system code, diameter and slope (`ES Ø100  i = 1,0 %`), and conduit tags list their circuits. Devices use the usual plan symbols: an outlet triangle filled by height, a ceiling light circle with its circuit and power, the switch, the board. Ordinary plans leave pipes, conduits and devices out. Pipes and conduits show in 3D, sections and elevations.

Devices are written as `IfcOutlet`, `IfcSwitchingDevice`, `IfcLightFixture` and `IfcElectricDistributionBoard`. Systems become `IfcDistributionSystem` and circuits `IfcDistributionCircuit`, grouping their elements. CI validates an MEP fixture.

Elbows (with their angle), couplings, reducers, tees and crosses show in plumbing plans as dots in the system's color, and conduit junctions as boxes. Two-letter shortcuts come from the same table and say why when they cannot run.
- Two-tone interface icons: strokes in the ink tone and soft fills in the theme's tint, with badges for commands that create (new views, components), import, export or remove. Theme colors are tokens with a dark set alongside the light one, ready for a dark theme.
- The ribbon collapses to its tabs (Ctrl+F1, a double click on a tab or the arrow at the end of the tabs) and the choice is remembered; while collapsed, a click on a tab shows its panel over the views until a command runs. When the window is narrower than a tab's groups, they scroll sideways with arrows at the ends.
- Command palette (Ctrl+Shift+P, or the magnifier after the ribbon tabs): finds interface commands, views to open, plans and schedules to create, and engine commands by name, description or shortcut (accents and case aside); engine commands open a form with their JSON arguments and run in the open project.
- Accessibility: the custom ribbon, palette, browser, home screen, dialog and property widgets report their role, name and state to screen readers. Interface tests drive the ribbon and the palette headlessly (egui_kittest), and screenshot tests of every ribbon tab, the narrow and collapsed ribbon, a rich tooltip, the palette and an icon gallery in both themes run on request.
- Interface guide (`docs/interface.md`, `docs/interface.pt-BR.md`): ribbon, tooltips, badges, collapsing, the command palette and its engine forms, keyboard, accessibility, and for developers how to add interface commands and icons and run the interface tests.

### Changed

- Cut hatching belongs to its element, so it highlights with it and joined geometry can trim it; the 3D boolean engine moved from `cc_ifc` to `cc_geom` (`cc_geom::csg`).
- Ribbon: *Load component* and *New component* use the component icon with an import or new badge, the sheet exports carry an export badge, and new views a new badge; a two-letter shortcut that cannot run says why in the status bar instead of doing nothing.
- `.ccad` format version 6 (joined geometry, structural columns, groups; version 5 added text notes); older files still open.
- The desktop app runs on the engine: its drawing tools, modify tools, properties, dialogs, clipboard, dimensions, document actions and undo/redo change the model through the same commands as the command line and MCP. Edits that would leave the model invalid are refused with a message; dragged values remain one undo step.
- Deleting a level also unconnects columns and stairs whose top was attached to it.
- IFC import without dropping classes: every physical object of an IFC2X3/IFC4 file comes in. Products become native elements whenever a native element holds their geometry exactly; everything else (stairs, railings, members, plates, curtain walls, MEP segments and fittings, terrain, curved walls, classes from other schemas) becomes a generic component that keeps its IFC class, predefined type, object type and exact geometry, and is exported back as the same class (with its IFC4 type object, or as a named `IfcBuildingElementProxy` for classes IFC4 lacks). `IfcGrid` axes become grid lines.
- In-house IFC geometry engine: placements (local and grid), polylines, indexed curves with arcs, circles, ellipses, trimmed and composite curves, B-splines (rational too); every parametric profile, arbitrary profiles with voids, center-line, composite, derived and mirrored profiles; extrusions, revolutions, swept disks and directrix sweeps; CSG primitives; boolean union, difference and intersection; half-space clipping; faceted and advanced B-reps; face sets and mapped items. Plane angle, area and volume units are read.
- Openings (`IfcRelVoidsElement`) are cut from imported generic objects with in-house mesh booleans; slabs, coverings, roofs, columns and beams with openings come in as generic objects so their holes are kept.
- Property and quantity sets of imported generic objects (their own and their type's) are kept with their IFC value types, converted to SI units, listed in Properties and written back on IFC export.
- Components can hold fixed mesh pieces; plans draw them cut at the cut plane with the edges seen from above.
- Properties show the IFC class, predefined type and object type of imported objects, and the import message counts the objects brought in as generic objects.
- `export_ifc` example accepts an `.ifc` file (import and export again); CI validates a hand-written IFC4 fixture covering many classes with IfcOpenShell.

Match Type Properties (MA) picks an element and gives its type to others of the same category.

- Associative dimensions: in plans each end of a new dimension follows the element it was placed on (walls, grids, columns, beams, doors, windows, components), through moves, rotations, stretches and undo; angular dimensions also keep their vertex at the intersection of the measured lines; in elevations and sections ends follow elements (with their height from the element's base or wall top) and level lines, and editing a dimension between levels raises or lowers the upper level; the dimension line stays where it was placed. Copies follow the copied elements; deleting an element frees the end. The sample project's dimensions follow its grids.
- Dimension values are editable: click the text of a selected dimension, double-click a dimension or type in Properties; in plans the new value moves what the dimension touches at its moving end (crossing elements translate, walls/beams/grids along it stretch), in one undo step. Angular values rotate; radial and diameter values are read-only.
- Dimension kinds: linear (horizontal or vertical by where the line is placed), angular (between two picked lines), radial and diameter (picked on any arc or circle in the view), besides aligned; one shared layout draws them in views, sheets and tool previews. - Selecting objects with the Select tool (in any view) opens the Modify tab.
- Zoom menu on the view, sheet and 3D zoom button: zoom in region (drag a window), zoom out (2x), zoom to fit, zoom all to fit, zoom sheet size (paper millimeters at screen size) and previous/next pan/zoom; the last command chosen becomes the button's action. In 3D views the window zoom frames the dragged region and the history keeps camera positions.
- Interface icons redrawn as SVG line drawings (`assets/icons/ui`), rasterized with resvg at the exact pixel size and aligned to the pixel grid, so they stay crisp at any scale.
- Ribbon: long labels wrap onto two lines, and panels with many tools stack small icon buttons with tooltips.

- Parametric components: definitions made of boxes, cylinders and extruded profiles sized by expressions over named type or instance parameters; a component editor with live expression checking and plan/front previews; a starter library (table, chair, bed, sofa, toilet, washbasin, wardrobe); the *Component* tool (CM) with rotation; instance parameter overrides in Properties; components in 3D, plans, elevations, sections, legends and schedules; `.ccomp` files to save and load components; IFC export and import as `IfcFurniture`, `IfcSanitaryTerminal`, `IfcLightFixture` and `IfcBuildingElementProxy`, keeping parametric types and instance values on a round trip.

- Room, grid and dimension types; projects created earlier gain default types when opened.
