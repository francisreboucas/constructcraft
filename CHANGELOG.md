# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The parametric steel building. *Structure → Framing → Steel Building* makes a portal-frame shed from a span, eave height, roof slope (gable or mono), bays and bay spacing: columns, rafters, purlins and girts as ordinary columns and beams, in one undo step (`steel_building_create`). The members are regenerated in place with their ids kept when the building's properties change, and a member edited or deleted by hand is detached and left alone (*Reset Members* takes it back). Plans draw the building's footprint dashed; IFC export writes the members as columns and beams.
- The Structure tab by material and Grid Frame. A *Material* group (Concrete, Steel, Timber; kept between sessions) swaps the tab's material groups, and its Column and Beam tools use types of that material, creating a default one from the catalog when there is none (`structural_type_for`; `StructuralMaterial` and `Project::structural_material` read a type's material from its profile). *Structure → Framing → Grid Frame* places columns on every storey and beams on every level of a range over the grids, with optional secondary beams spaced across the bays, in one undo step (`grid_frame_create`, at most 2000 members per run).
- Structural profiles for columns and beams. Column and beam types can point to a parametric profile (`Project::profiles`: steel, cold-formed, timber and concrete shapes such as I, H, channel, angle, tube and sketched outlines, with built-in catalogs) instead of a fixed section; profile section properties (area, inertia, modulus, radius of gyration, linear mass) are computed from the outline. Columns and beams are built, drawn (cut, with their arcs at fine detail) and swept from the profile, beams frame into column hulls, tags show a steel or cold-formed profile's name, and schedules have *Profile* and *Linear mass* (kg/m, from the material's density) fields. The template's steel material now has a density of 7850 kg/m³ (new projects only). The Profile Manager (*Structure → Profiles*, *Manage → Profiles*) lists, edits, duplicates and sketches profiles and imports the built-in catalogs (W, HEA/HEB/IPE, tubes, NBR 6355 cold-formed, sawn timber, glulam); the same operations are engine commands (`profiles_list`, `profile_get`, `profile_create`, `profile_set`, `profile_duplicate`, `profile_delete`, `profile_catalog`, `profile_import`, `profile_type_create`, and `type_set` `profile`). IFC export writes parametric profile definitions where the standard has one and arbitrary profiles otherwise, with an `IfcMaterialProfileSet` per type, and import makes native types only when it holds the geometry exactly. The analytical model export is now format 2: a member's `section` is `{ profile, area, ix, iy, j, width, height }`. Files are now format 27.
- Parametric trusses for steel and timber. *Structure → Framing → Truss* (in the steel and timber groups) takes the two ends of the span line and makes a truss from its shape (flat, mono-pitch or gable outline), web pattern (Pratt, Howe, Warren, king post, queen post and Fink), height, panels, and copies at a spacing. Chords, diagonals and posts are ordinary beams and columns regenerated in place with their ids kept when a parameter changes, and detached when edited by hand; the properties palette shows the parameters and member types, and *Reset Members* makes the truss manage every member again.
- Parametric bracing for steel and timber frames. *Structure → Bracing* braces the bay between two selected columns (taking their base and height), or starts a tool that takes the two column axes; the pattern is X, diagonal (with *Flip* and *Alternate* per storey), V, chevron (inverted V) or K, over any number of storeys of a storey height (`bracing_create`). The braces are ordinary beams regenerated in place with their ids kept when a parameter changes, and detached when edited by hand; the properties palette shows the parameters and the brace type, and *Reset Members* makes the bracing manage every brace again. It replaces the *X Bracing* button (`braces_between_columns` stays as a command).
- Light steel and wood framing of walls. *Structure → Framing → Light Steel Frame* (steel) and *Wood Frame* (timber) frame the selected walls, or the active level's, from a dialog (system, stud spacing, member types, double top plate for wood, blocking). The members are studs, tracks or plates, king and jack studs, headers, sills, cripples and optional blocking, all ordinary columns and beams; they are regenerated with their ids kept when the wall, its doors and windows or the framing change, and detached when edited by hand (*Reset Members* brings them back). Adds the `wall_framing_create` command and the framing's properties in the palette.
- Steel and timber connections: base plates with anchor bolts, end plates, fin plates, bolted timber joints with washers and hangers at column bases and beam ends, sized from the member's section and following it. *Structure → Connections* in the steel and timber groups places them on the selected members or the active level's (`connections_create`); plate mass, bolt count and diameter are schedule fields; IFC export writes element assemblies of plates and mechanical fasteners.
- Arc beams, and reinforcement that follows the arc (#279). `beam_create` takes `mid`, `bulge` or `radius` (with `major_arc`) as `wall_create` does, and the *Beam* tool has *Line* and *Arc* modes (start, end, then a point on the arc or a typed radius). An arc beam is level, frames into columns and other beams at its ends (each side stopping where it leaves the column), shows its *Arc angle*, radius and length along the arc in the properties palette, has the curvature handle, snaps by its ends, middle and arc, and is trimmed, split, offset and mirrored on its circle; its volume is exact. IFC export writes a rectangular one as its outline of true arcs extruded, and import brings it back as an arc beam. In an arc beam top, bottom and side bars follow the arc, each as long as the arc at its own radius, and stirrups are radial, spaced along the axis.
- More beam reinforcement (#279). Top and bottom bars that do not fit side by side (a clear distance of the bar diameter, at least 2 cm) go in a second layer; `rebar_create` has a `sides` layout for side (skin) bars on both side faces, by `count` per face or by a `spacing` over the height (*Add side bars* in the properties palette); and bars along a beam longer than `max_length` (default: the 12 m stock length, 40' in imperial projects; 0 keeps whole bars) are made of pieces lapping by `lap` (default: 40 bar diameters), the splices of neighbouring bars staggered. Schedules count and measure the pieces.
- Annular sector column sections and column reinforcement by face (#280). The Profile Manager and `profile_create` have an *Annular sector* shape (`annular_sector`: inner and outer radius and the angle it turns), the piece of a cylindrical wall a column continues, with its exact area and volume; L, T and U columns are sketched profiles. `rebar_create` gives a column's `perimeter` bars face by face with `faces` (a count for each face of the section, the corner bars shared, at even angles on curved faces; `element_get` lists a column's `derived.faces`) or `face_spacing`; its `stirrups` follow the outline of any section, curved faces as curves; a `ties` layout adds hooked cross-ties from each bar between corners to the bar opposite; `max_length` and `lap` cut a column's vertical bars in lifts that lap; and `same_type` gives the set to every column (or beam) of the host's type.
- Wall reinforcement, for straight and arc walls (#281). `wall_rebar_create` gives walls horizontal bars up the wall (ring bars in an arc wall, each as long as the arc at its own radius), vertical bars along it and ties between the faces, near both faces or one, with a cover per face. Horizontal bars close up towards the base by `zones` of spacing; vertical bars may cover part of the height, start between the bars of another set (the additional bars of a zone) and have their ends bent across the wall and along it (an L hook, or a bar turned into the slab and up again); either family may be the outer layer. Bars lie inside the wall's structural core, stop around doors and windows, run on through an end joined to another wall, and are lap spliced past `max_length`, neighbouring splices staggered. A structural wall's properties show its *Reinforcement*, with a *Typical Reinforcement* button. The sets are listed in rebar schedules, drawn in 3D rebar views and written to IFC.
- Area reinforcement for slabs and rafts of any boundary (#282). `slab_rebar_create` gives floors and foundation slabs a mesh (straight bars in two directions at an `angle`, each with its type and spacing) or ring and radial bars (with shorter intermediate radial bars towards the edge), in the top and bottom layers. Every bar is clipped to the slab's true boundary less the cover, arcs and circles as drawn, and around its openings and shafts, so each has its own length; `element_get` lists a set's bars by length. Mesh bars may be bent at the slab's edge (`hook`: down, up, or out into the wall), bars longer than `max_length` are lap spliced with neighbouring splices staggered, and a slab with openings gets trimming bars along each side of every opening, reaching an anchorage past it, with a diagonal across each corner. Floors have a *Structural* checkbox (`floor_create` `structural`); a structural floor's or a foundation slab's properties show its *Reinforcement*, with a *Typical Reinforcement* button, and without a list the command reinforces those slabs only.
- Ring strip footings with a beam on top, their reinforcement, and curved slab foundations (#283). A strip footing type can be an inverted T (*Beam on the footing* in Type Properties, or `haunch` in `type_set`): a footing slab of an edge thickness and a thickness at the faces of the beam, its top sloping between them, under a beam of a width and height; straight or along an arc, with its exact volume, in plans, sections, 3D views and IFC. `strip_rebar_create` reinforces strip footings: bars across the footing (radial in an arc strip), the top ones following the slopes and turning down at the edges to close them, bars along it (ring bars, each as long as the arc at its own radius), and for the beam stirrups over the full height of the section, top, bottom and side bars. A foundation slab drawn with `curves` keeps its arcs and circles, so a circular raft has its true area and volume and the slab reinforcement ends on its curve. A strip footing's properties show its *Reinforcement*.
- An imperial project template next to the metric one (#320). *New* (quick access, Ctrl+N, the home screen) opens a New Project dialog with two cards, *Metric System* and *Imperial System*; the one picked last is offered first next time. The imperial template has the same levels, types and views as the metric one, with feet and inches, types in round inches and names that state no metric size (`project_new` takes `template`, `project_info` reports `system`). In an imperial project drawing scales read the customary way (`1/4" = 1'-0"`; `view_create`, `view_set`, `template_set` and `callout_create` also take the scale as text, `view_get` adds `scale_text`), sheets come in the ANSI A to E and architectural A to E sizes (new sheets are ANSI B), sizes on paper and pen weights read in inches, roof, ramp and pipe slopes read as a rise per 12 (`6:12`), schedules, tags, type properties, the Profile Manager, the material browser and check messages show inches, lb, lb/ft and ksi, and what commands and tools make outside the template (doors, ramps, baseboards, trays, curtain grids, rebar, terrain) gets round imperial sizes. Metric projects read as before; engine parameters and results, files, IFC and DXF stay SI. Files are now format 28.
- Ring bars lap at the joints between the parts of a ring (#281, #283, #279). A cylinder is made of two or more walls, a ring footing of two or more strips, a ring beam of several beams, and the bars along each stopped at the joints. With `lap_at_joins`, `wall_rebar_create` (horizontal bars), `strip_rebar_create` (the bars along the strip and its beam's) and `rebar_create` (the bars along a beam) run them half a lap past each end where another wall, strip or beam carries on in the same line, on their own line or circle, so the bars of the two lap by `lap` there. Ends at corners, tees and columns are left as they were.
- Wall ties take the angles of their two hooks (#281): `wall_rebar_create` `ties.hooks`, 90 to 180 degrees at the exterior and the interior face (135 at both by default, as before), counted in the tie's length.
- Overlapping stirrups and lifts at given heights in columns (#280). `rebar_create` `stirrups` with `overlapping` gives a column whose section has only square corners several closed stirrups, one around each of the rectangles it is made of (two in an L or a T, three in a U), instead of one following the whole outline; and `perimeter` with `lifts` cuts the vertical bars at those heights above the base, the bars of each lift lapping `lap` past the next, instead of in equal lifts under `max_length`.
- Bar marks, bending schedules, bar tags and the bar length check (#286). Every reinforcing bar has a mark (N1, N2…) naming its shape: bars of the same diameter, steel grade, legs and bends, within 5 mm and 1° (read from either end or mirrored), share one, whatever host they lie in; bars along arcs are read as arcs with their radius. Marks are numbered by partition, then diameter and length from the largest; a set can carry its own mark (`bar_mark`); freezing keeps the marks shown when the model changes (new shapes are numbered after them), and renumbering starts again from a chosen prefix and number. Reinforcement sets have a `partition` (the part of the structure they belong to, a parameter of every reinforcement command and a schedule field), and bar types a steel `grade` and an optional `mass_per_meter`, which weights use. A bending schedule is a reinforcement schedule with one row per mark: mark, diameter, grade, a sketch of the shape with the length of each leg (arcs with their radius), the length of one bar, the count, the total length and the weight, rows of bars longer than the stock length (12 m by default) noted; under it, a summary per diameter and grade with an optional waste percentage. Totals add the bars exactly. It can be limited to partitions or grouped by them, placed on sheets, exported, and written to CSV with the shapes as codes (`L 30-470`, `stirrup 14x44`). Bar tags in plans, elevations and sections write a set as `25 N1 Ø12.5 c/12 C=500` (with `2x` for sets on both faces) and draw its bars in that view; they go with their set. IFC export writes a set's partition, mark and grade in `ConstructCAD_Rebar`, and import reads them back. Commands: `bar_marks_list`, `bar_marks_update`, `bar_marks_renumber`, `bar_marking_set`, `bar_length_check`, `bending_schedule_create`, `bar_tag_create`.
- Piles and pile caps (#284). A pile element (category *Piles*) has a round or square section and a kind (bored, continuous flight auger, micro or root pile, precast or steel driven, other) from its type, a cut-off and a toe level (or a length) and an embedment into the cap above. A pile cap is a foundation type of the *Pile cap* kind whose layout places its piles: one, two, three in a triangle, four in a square, a line of any number, or any number on a circle under a round cap or a ring cap; spacing, edge distance, cap thickness, pile type, length and embedment are type parameters, and the cap's outline follows (a three-pile cap is a triangle with its corners cut square). Piles are made with their cap in the same undo step and follow it: they move, turn and are copied and deleted with it, change when its type's layout or count changes, and go back when moved alone. *Pile caps under columns* places a cap under each column turned as the column, by an angle, or radially about a center, so caps under columns on a circle point outward; tie beams join consecutive caps, straight or as arcs about a center, flush with their tops, and stop at the caps' outlines. Plans draw caps like other foundations and piles dashed with a center mark; 3D views and sections show round piles and caps with smooth faces; volumes are exact. A pile schedule lists mark, type, diameter, cut-off and toe levels, length, volume and cap, and foundation schedules count each cap's piles. IFC export writes `IfcPile` with an `IfcPileType` and caps as `IfcFooting` `PILE_CAP`, with this program's data in `ConstructCAD_Pile` and `ConstructCAD_PileCap`, so its own files come back with native caps and piles; another program's piles that are plain vertical round or square extrusions come in as piles. Commands: `pile_create`, `pile_type_create`, `pile_cap_type_create`, `pile_cap_create`, `pile_caps_under_columns`, `tie_beams_create`. No format change.
- Schedule properties in five parts: Fields, Filter, Sorting/Grouping, Formatting and Appearance. A schedule's properties list them, each with an *Edit…* button that opens its definition on that page. Filters compare a field with a value (equals, contains, begins with, greater, less, has a value… the comparisons view filters have), numbers as numbers, and a row must pass every one; fields that are not shown filter too. Sorting runs in up to four levels, each ascending or descending, and a level may group its rows under a heading, over a footer with the group's count and totals, and apart by a blank line. With *One row per element* off, the elements alike in every sorting level share a row: values that differ read as `<varies>`, totaled columns add up, and the new *Count* field says how many. Each column takes its own heading and alignment, and may be hidden (still sorting and filtering) or totaled or not. The appearance (grid lines, outline, the title and header rows, the three text sizes) is how the table is drawn on sheets and exports. Commands: `schedule_get` and `schedule_set`; older schedules load unchanged, their single filter and sort field reading as the first filter and level.
- Sun studies. Plans, ceiling plans, 3D views, elevations and sections can have a sun (*View → Graphics → Sun Settings*, or the *Sun* row of a view's properties): a still moment, a study over one day or several days (every 15, 30 or 45 minutes or hour, or every day, week or month, between two times or from sunrise to sunset, the frame shown chosen in the dialog), or a light from a direction given by hand, from true north or relative to the view. The sun's position comes from the date, the time and the site location, local time following the longitude's time zone or one set by hand (`site_set` `utc_offset`). Built-in presets (the solstices and equinoxes, the whole year, two lights) and the project's own named presets set the mode in one click. The sun path diagram (a compass ring turned to true north, the day's path with its hours, sunrise, sunset and the date, and the sun) is drawn over plans, elevations, sections and 3D views, and is not picked or cropped; shaded projected views and 3D views are lit by the sun, and 3D views cast its shadows on the model and, optionally, on the plane of a level. The view cube's compass shows true north. Commands: `view_set`/`view_get` `sun`, `sun_position`, `sun_frames`, `sun_preset_save`, `sun_presets_list`, `sun_preset_rename`, `sun_preset_delete`. No format change.

### Changed

- *New Component* opens in the component environment. The ribbon button asks for a name and the kind of the component and shows the parameters that kind starts with (width, depth and height for furniture, casework, plumbing and electrical; diameter and height for lighting; the opening's width, height and sill for doors and windows; none for a generic component, whose parameters are yours to add); *Create* makes it and opens the environment, *Cancel Editing* removes it and *Finish* picks the tool that places it. `component_create` takes a `kind` in place of `def` for the same starting components. The older one-window editor and its *Quick Edit…* button in a component type's properties are gone: an existing component's opening, window or door operation, script piece and the void and plan-only flags of its pieces are set with the `component_*` commands and `type_set`.
- `truss_create` now makes a parametric truss element and returns `{id, members}` instead of a group of loose members (`created`, `group` and `name` are gone); default types come from the material's catalog rows.
- Dialogs are moved by dragging their header, and open centered again the next time (#312). A dialog widens to fit its footer buttons, and a table row's value is clipped to its cell instead of widening the table.
- Large models are faster to work in (#303, #305). 2D views and sheets keep their painted drawing between frames and redraw only what a selection changes; picking, hover and snapping (to the model and to imported drawings) look only at what is near the pointer; selecting in a 3D view draws a highlight over the cached scene instead of rebuilding it; imported meshes are shared between copies and undo steps instead of copied. Model builds trace all the rooms of a level in one wall arrangement, index each element's solids and hosted doors and windows, reuse opening pieces per type and wall thickness, and order projected faces with a heap; schedule views lay out only their visible rows, and repairing a graph's wires no longer rescans every wire per node.
- The largest picture a project keeps is now 64 million pixels (it was 200 million).

### Fixed

- A circular foundation slab's volume in `element_get`, schedules and IFC quantities is the circle's, not that of the chords it is drawn with, and `element_get` gives a foundation's `area`. A column's or beam's `section` `area` and mass per meter (and the *Linear mass* schedule field) count the arcs of its profile whole: a round, tubular or annular sector section was reported up to 0.6% short.
- Surface patterns and material textures on curved surfaces (#314). A pattern could jump inside the facets of a curved wall or roof; patterns and textures now run on from one smooth facet to the next, and patterns on sloped or diagonal flat faces are drawn at true scale.
- Dialogs whose contents lay outside them or whose footer buttons lay over each other (#312): Type Properties (the component definition buttons), Visibility/Graphics, Filters, View Templates, Materials and the Component Library. Checkboxes in table cells keep a visible box.
- `skp_import` and `skp_component_load` no longer hang on a file whose definitions place each other many times over, or themselves (#303): such a file is refused as too large.
- A deeply nested SVG picked as a title block picture no longer overflows the stack (#305).
- Cached drawings of deleted views are dropped (#303).

### Security

- Files are checked for size before they are read (#303, #305): projects over 1 GB, IFC and component files over 512 MiB and macros over 32 MiB are refused with an error. An IFC property set reads at most 10,000 properties and refuses a complex property that holds itself.
- Import commands that read a file named by a parameter (`cad_import`, `image_import`, `component_load`, `skp_import`, `obj_import`, `gltf_import`, `fill_pattern_import`, `material_texture_set`, `site_context_import`, `graph_run` and others) refuse the path in a plugin run without the `files` permission and in a graph run without `allow_files`; their inline forms (text, image data) keep working (#303).
- The Lua sandbox bounds memory and time inside library functions too (#303): runs have a wall-clock limit (60 s; component scripts 5 s), string functions are charged by the size of their text, `string.rep` refuses results over 16 MB, and pattern matching is step-counted, so a backtracking pattern stops at the budget. Component script and graph Lua chunks load in text mode only.
- Schedule CSV cells that look like formulas (starting with `=`, `@`, a tab, or `+`/`-` without being a number) are written as text, so a spreadsheet does not run them (#303).
- The app's network requests and their redirects stay on https, the updater checks the installer's SHA-256 again just before running it, and the CI workflows pin their actions to commit SHAs (#303).

## [0.11.1] - 2026-11-11

### Added

- Project browser organization (#278). *View → Browser Organization* (also in the menu of a group in the project browser) keeps named schemes that group the browser's views by view type, level, discipline or phase, nested in the order chosen: a scheme by view type and level puts each level's plans in a group under *Floor Plans* and *Ceiling Plans*, so the plans of a level stay together whatever their names. Views without a value for a criterion (a section has no level) stay beside its groups, groups fold and show how many views they hold, and the list by view type alone stays the default. The schemes are saved with the project; `browser_scheme_create`, `browser_scheme_set`, `browser_scheme_delete`, `browser_scheme_use`, `browser_schemes_list` and `browser_tree` do the same from the command line and MCP.

### Changed

- *Manage → Levels* edits levels by storey height and adds levels next to one (#297). Each level shows the *Height to Next Level*: a new height moves every level above by the difference, so the other storeys keep theirs. An elevation moves its level alone and stays between the level below and the level above, and both values are taken when the field is left, so a number being typed no longer reorders the list under the cursor. *Add Above* and *Add Below* add a storey beside the picked level, of the height of the storey next to it, with its plans, pushing the levels beyond it away. `level_set` takes `height`, and `level_create` takes `above` or `below` with `height`.
- A level and its views are named apart (#278). Renaming a level no longer renames the plans that had its name, and renaming a plan no longer renames its level: a level may have many views, and the plans made with it only start with its name. `view_set` no longer takes `rename_level`.
- The ribbon's large buttons keep a least width and the same room at each side of their labels, so the icons of a group stand evenly apart, and labels also wrap after a slash or a hyphen. *Blocks as components* leaves the *Insert* tab for *Preferences → General → Import*, and is now kept between sessions.

## [0.11.0] - 2026-11-11

### Added

- Dimension type parameters. *Type Properties* of a dimension type sets the mark at the ends of the dimension line (tick, filled or open arrow, dot and the other leader symbols, or none) with its size and line weight, the line weight and color of the dimension and witness lines, the witness line gap and extension, how far the dimension line runs past the witness lines, the text size and its distance from the line, a prefix and a suffix around the value, and an opaque background behind the text. Radial, diameter and ordinate dimensions keep their arrows; the color applies to lines and marks. Types made before keep their look. `type_set` takes them as `params.style`.
- glTF import. *Insert → Import glTF…* brings a glTF 2.0 model (`.glb`, or `.gltf` with its buffers embedded or in files next to it) into the project on the active view's level: each node of the scene's root, with everything below it, becomes a component element whose type holds its triangles as fixed mesh pieces, one per material, inserted at the center of its footprint and the bottom of its extent; node transforms (matrices or translation, rotation and scale) are followed down the hierarchy, and mirrored placements keep their faces outward. glTF is in meters with Y up, so the dialog only asks whether to bring the whole scene in as one component. Colors come from the materials' base color, and missing materials are added by name. Files that require compressed meshes (Draco, meshopt) are refused with the extension's name. The import is one undo step. Command: `gltf_import` (`merge`).
- Doors and windows placed by typed distances and drawing snaps (#250, #251). While placing a door or window in a plan, temporary dimensions run from its center to both ends of the wall (along the arc for arc walls); typing a value places the center that far from the wall's start, and Tab switches to the distance from its end (Enter places it, Backspace edits, Esc drops the value; a distance past the end of the wall is refused). The opening's center or either edge also lines up with lines of imported drawings that cross the wall (axes, jamb lines), even where the wall covers them, and with their end, middle and center points within reach; the opening stays on the wall with the pointer further off it. Every drawing tool now also snaps to the plan underlay.
- OBJ import. *Insert → Import OBJ…* brings a Wavefront `.obj` model into the project on the active view's level: each object (or group, in a file without objects) becomes a component element whose type holds its faces as fixed mesh pieces, one per material, inserted at the center of its footprint and the bottom of its extent. Colors come from the `.mtl` library next to the file (`Kd`), and missing materials are added by name. Since OBJ does not say, a dialog asks for the file's units (m, cm, mm, in, ft) and up axis (Y, the usual one, or Z), and can bring the whole file in as one component at its origin. Faces that are not flat are split into triangles; normals, lines and points are ignored. The import is one undo step. Command: `obj_import` (`units`, `z_up`, `merge`).
- Imported models bring their textures, colors and opacity. SketchUp materials keep their texture, with its tile size, and their opacity; OBJ materials take the picture of `map_Kd` (found by name next to the model) and `d`/`Tr`; glTF materials take their base color texture (from the file, a data URI or a file next to it) and, when blended, their opacity. A textured glTF model no longer comes in white: a material's color is its color times its picture's average. The picture becomes the material's texture, shown in the *Textures* visual style; since textures are laid on each face's plane, the file's texture coordinates set how many meters a repeat spans rather than where it falls.
- French doors and double doors (#249). A door component's opening has an *Operation*: one leaf hinged left (the default) or right, or two leaves hinged on either jamb meeting in the middle. Plans and legends draw one swing per leaf (two symmetric arcs for a double door), elevations and sections a triangle per leaf, and IFC export writes the operation type on `IfcDoor` and `IfcDoorType` (`SINGLE_SWING_LEFT`, `SINGLE_SWING_RIGHT`, `DOUBLE_DOOR_SINGLE_SWING`, a flipped single door taking the other hand) with an `IfcDoorPanelProperties` per leaf; import reads it back. The template adds a French door 80x220 and a double French door 140x220: glazed leaves of stiles and rails around a pane over a solid bottom rail whose height is a parameter of the type (`BottomRail`, 40 cm). `door_type_create` makes swing or French door types of one or two leaves at any size; the component editor picks the operation. The showcase macro's ids move by the two new template types.
- Windows whose panels open, and opening symbols in plans, elevations and sections (#239). A window component's opening has an *Operation*: fixed (the default), casement hinged left or right, double casement, top-hung, horizontal pivot, vertical pivot or sliding (two panels). Plans draw side-hung panels open square to the wall on the side the window opens to, with a dashed swing arc (the element's flips turn the side and the hand), sliding panels offset on their tracks, and a window wholly above the cut plane dashed, jambs too; legends draw the same symbols. Elevations and sections draw the opening direction on doors and windows: dashed lines whose apex points at the hinge (doors, casements) or the head (top-hung), crossed lines and a dash-dot axis for pivots, arrows for sliding panels; whatever stands in front hides them. The template adds *Casement window 60x100*, *Double casement window 120x120*, *Top-hung window 60x60* and *Sliding window 150x120*; the existing window types stay fixed. IFC export writes the type's `IfcWindowPanelProperties` (`FIXEDCASEMENT`, `SIDEHUNGLEFTHAND`, `SIDEHUNGRIGHTHAND`, `TOPHUNG`, `PIVOTHORIZONTAL`, `PIVOTVERTICAL`, `SLIDINGHORIZONTAL`, with the panel position) and the partitioning (`SINGLE_PANEL` or `DOUBLE_PANEL_VERTICAL`) on the type and the window; import reads them back, matching types by operation too. Set it in the component editor (*Quick Edit*) or with `component_create`/`type_set` (`def.opening.operation`). No format change.
- Strip footings and ground beams that follow arc walls (#22). A strip footing can carry its own arc, like walls do: `strip_footing_create` takes `mid`, `bulge` or `radius`, and *Ground Beams Under Walls* (`strip_footings_under_walls`) now gives an arc wall an arc strip along its body centerline instead of skipping it. Arc strips meet straight ones and other arcs at the true crossings of their faces, plans draw the curve (as short chords) with the usual dashed and cut conventions, sections cut the curved body (smooth facets in 3D), and the area, volume and length along the arc are exact; `element_get` shows the radius, `element_set` bends one through `bulge` (an arc too tight for the strip's width is refused), and a mirror turns it the other way. The pad footing tool, with a strip type, draws strips like walls, in *Line* or *Arc* mode; strip properties show the arc angle, length and radius, and a selected arc strip has a curvature handle at its middle, like arc walls; the options bar names the tool *Strip Footing* while a strip type is chosen. IFC export writes an arc strip as an `IfcFooting` (`STRIP_FOOTING`) whose body is its outline of true arcs extruded; IFC import still brings strip footings back as components. Files are now format 26.
- Custom fill patterns and `.pat` import (#226). *Manage → Fill Patterns* (also from the material browser and a filled region type's fill) lists the built-in patterns and the project's own with swatches, imports `.pat` hatch pattern files (pick which patterns come in; kind, units, scale and name clashes), and edits a pattern's name, kind (drafting: sized on paper; model: real-world size that scales with the view), scale and line families (angle, origin, shift, spacing, dashes) over a live preview; a pattern in use is deleted with a replacement. Materials (cut and surface), filled region types and view overrides use them; plans, sections, elevations, legends, regions and detail components hatch with them and every export gets their lines; 3D views show the material's plain color. Commands: `fill_patterns_list`, `fill_pattern_get`, `fill_pattern_create`, `fill_pattern_set`, `fill_pattern_duplicate`, `fill_pattern_delete`, `fill_pattern_import`, `fill_pattern_export`. Files are now format 25.
- Lightning protection and structured cabling (#34). *MEP → Lightning Protection* draws conductors (`LC`: on the roof surface, down conductors as vertical runs, earthing below the level, with a conductor type of diameter and material), places air terminals on the roof (`AT`) and earth electrodes, lays an earth ring around the walls, and checks how the air termination covers the roofs for a protection class (I to IV) by the rolling sphere and mesh methods. *MEP → Communications* places racks, each with its structured cabling (a new data system kind), and data outlets (`DO`) cabled to them, with jacks per outlet; *Cabling* lists per rack the outlets, cables, estimated cable lengths and the cables over 90 m. New lightning protection and communications plans draw them with their symbols; conduits and cable trays carry the cabling; schedules list conductors by role and length and each outlet's cable length; *Systems* groups circuits, cabling and lightning protection. IFC export writes `IfcCableSegment` (`CONDUCTORSEGMENT`), `IfcProtectiveDevice` (air terminals, earth electrodes), `IfcOutlet` (`DATAOUTLET`), `IfcCommunicationsAppliance` (racks) and `IfcDistributionSystem` (`LIGHTNINGPROTECTION`, `DATA`). Commands: `lps_conductor_create`, `lps_conductor_type_create`, `earth_ring_create`, `air_terminal_place`, `earth_electrode_place`, `lps_check`, `rack_place`, `data_outlet_place`, `cabling_list`, `device_type_create`.
- Cable trays and valves (#33). *MEP → Electrical → Cable Tray* (`TY`) draws cable tray runs like conduits, carrying circuits; their types set the width, height, construction (ladder, solid bottom or perforated) and material. Trays join other trays with derived elbows, tees and crosses, show in electrical plans as wide as they are (rungs on ladder trays, a tag with the section and circuits) and in 3D as open channels, schedule their width, height and length, and export to IFC as `IfcCableCarrierSegment` (`CABLETRAYSEGMENT`). *MEP → Plumbing → Valve* (`VL`) places a gate, ball, check or globe valve on a clicked pipe: it takes the pipe's diameter and system, is a node of its network, moves, is copied and is deleted with the pipe, shows in plumbing plans with the usual symbol and in 3D with its body and handwheel or lever, and exports as `IfcValve`. Commands: `cable_tray_create`, `cable_tray_type_create`, `valve_place`, `valve_type_create`.
- Design options (#35). Option sets hold alternatives for part of the model, each with a primary option; elements belong to the main model or to one option, and doors, windows and other hosted elements follow their host. Each plan, elevation, section, 3D view and schedule shows the main model plus one option per set (automatic: the primary, or the option being edited), chosen in its properties or by its view template; elements of other options are left out of its drawing, schedule, wall joins and room boundaries. *Manage → Design Options* creates, renames and deletes sets and options, picks the primary and merges it into the main model; *Manage → Active Option* picks the option new elements go into. IFC export writes the main model and the primary options, with `ConstructCAD_DesignOption`, read back on import. Commands: `options_list`, `option_set_create`, `option_set_rename`, `option_set_delete`, `option_create`, `option_set`, `option_delete`, `option_accept_primary`, `elements_option_set`, `view_options_set`, `option_active_set`. Files are now format 23.
- Schedules on sheets. A schedule is placed on a sheet like any view (dragged from the project browser, *Place on active sheet* or `sheet_place_view`) and drawn as a ruled table at its printed size: its name in the first row, the headers, a row per element its phase shows and the totals, right-aligned numbers. It follows the model, and the sheet's PDF, SVG, PNG and DXF carry it. *Export Image*, *Export DXF* and `view_export` also write a schedule alone as its table.
- Detailing reused across views (#159). What is drawn in a view (detail lines, filled and masking regions, text notes, dimensions, level marks, detail components, imported drawings) pastes into the active view when another 2D view is active, at the same coordinates, so a plot boundary or a buildable area drawn in the site plan can be reused in another plan. Dimensions pasted into another plan keep measuring the same elements. In the project browser, *Duplicate with Detailing* copies a view with what is drawn in it. Commands: `elements_paste` takes `view`, `view_duplicate` takes `detailing`.
- Material textures and painted faces (#36). A material's appearance can hold a picture (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP or WebP, up to 16 MB and 4096 × 4096 pixels, kept in the project file) with the real size of one repeat, an offset and a rotation: *Load Image…* in the *Appearance* tab of the material browser, with a thumbnail. In the *Textures* visual style, 3D views lay it on the material's faces at real size (horizontal faces along plan X and Y, walls and slopes level across and up), in a bounded texture array (16 textures of 512 × 512 pixels per view, with mipmaps); a picture that cannot be read shows the appearance color. **Paint** (*Modify → Geometry*, PA) paints the face of an element clicked in a 3D view (and the faces in its plane touching it, or the rest of a curved surface) with the material chosen in the options bar, shown in 3D views, elevations and sections; **Remove Paint** takes it off. A paint keeps a point and the outward normal of its face and, after the model changes, goes on the face turned within 20° of it that passes nearest the point; moving, rotating and mirroring the element carry it along. Commands: `material_texture_set`, `material_texture_clear`, `paint`, `paint_remove`, `paints_list`; `material_get` shows a summary of the picture. Files are format 23. IFC export does not write textures or paints yet.

### Changed

- Imported meshes have their coplanar faces merged (SketchUp, OBJ, glTF and IFC mesh pieces): the triangles a file splits a flat face into come back as one face, so elevations, sections and 3D views no longer draw diagonals across flat faces, and components hold fewer faces. Faces join only across shared edges in one plane (within 1° and 0.01 mm) and only while they stay one simple polygon, so a ring keeps its hole.
- A picture in the title block editor moves by dragging its body, not only its corner grip: grabbed anywhere inside, it keeps that point under the pointer and its corner snaps to the paper edges and planes, in one undo step.
- Curved surfaces shade smoothly in 3D views (#22): the facets of arc walls and other curved surfaces share their normals where they meet, so a curved wall no longer shows a step of light at each facet. Flat faces, and facets meeting at a sharp angle, keep their own shading.

### Fixed

- Floor plans draw columns over the walls they stand in or cross (#276): a column was drawn before the walls, so a wall's cut fill hid it, whatever the wall's cut pattern. A wall joined with an architectural column still cuts it.
- Renaming a level renames its plans, and renaming a plan named after its level renames the level (#274). Floor, ceiling, structural and MEP plans that carry the level's name follow it in the same undo step, so the project browser, the view tabs and the sheets show the new name; a plan named otherwise keeps its name. `view_set` takes `rename_level: false` to rename only the view.
- Recorded macros play on the active level and with their own elements (#275). A macro recorded on one plan drew on that level wherever it was played, and its doors and windows failed elsewhere because they named the walls the recording had made. Steps now refer to what earlier steps created (`{"$step": n}`), and the level of the plan recording them plays as the active plan's level (`{"$level": "active"}`); `ccad script` takes `level=` for it. Macros recorded before keep working as they are.
- IFC import keeps round slabs round (#264): a slab whose profile is an `IfcCircleProfileDef`, or an arbitrary profile made of polylines, trimmed circles and arc segments (with round voids too), comes in as a floor whose sketch holds the circles and arcs, and its area and volume are those of the true curves rather than of the 24-sided polygon it was drawn with (a slab of radius 2 m and 0.20 m now measures π·R²·t = 2.5133 m³, not 2.4847 m³).
- IFC import brings reinforcing bars in as reinforcement (#263): an `IfcReinforcingBar` drawn as swept disks becomes a set of shaped bars of the Reinforcement category (their center lines, bends included, where the file puts them, with arcs measured within 0.005% of their true length; no host), of one type per diameter and steel grade instead of one component type per bar, so reinforcement schedules count them, total their length and weight per diameter, and the 3D rebar view shows them. The bar's property sets stay with it, its mark comes from its tag or name, and IFC export writes it back as an `IfcReinforcingBar`. Bars drawn otherwise still come in as components, and identical type-less objects now share one component type.
- MCP clients that validate tool schemas against JSON Schema 2020-12 no longer drop `curtain_grid_set` (#261): tuple parameters are written as `prefixItems` (with `items` for draft 7 clients), and a test checks every command's schema.
- The inline editors of the visual programming graph's input nodes (number, integer, slider, text, yes/no) follow the canvas zoom: they no longer run past their nodes when zoomed out, and zooming in no longer grows the graph panel until its toolbar leaves the window.
- The *Plumbing Fixture* and *Electrical Device* tools list only their own component types in the options bar, not every component, and are named after what they place (#255).
- The component editor's *Quick Edit* fields no longer show `unknown name WallThickness` or a false error for reference planes (#238): they are checked with the same names the preview resolves with.
- An MCP client pointed at the app (`constructcraft mcp` or `--mcp`) gets the MCP server instead of a window that never answers (#265): the app runs the `ccad` next to it on the same standard input and output, or exits with a message when it is missing; unknown options are no longer taken for a project path. The automation guide and the README give the full path of `ccad.exe` on Windows for clients that do not see it on the PATH.
- IFC import no longer makes one type per element when an IFC type shares its name with a different project type (#247): the type it adds (`<name> (IFC)`) is reused by the following occurrences, a type that already carries the IFC type's GlobalId is reused, and types made from one IFC type with occurrences of different sizes keep unique ids.
- A property set back to its empty value in the properties palette or a dialog now changes (#225): a plan's *Underlay → Plan below/above* set back to *None* went back to the level it had, and the same happened to any value returning to none, an empty list or off (an option of a view, a type or an element). The edit now clears what it leaves out.
- The properties palette and the view template manager now set a plan's *Underlay → Plan below/above* back to *None* (#235). The fix for #225 covered the generic property edits, but these two send the underlay through their own patch, which left a cleared plan out (and failed when both were cleared); they now clear it the same way.
- SketchUp import follows OpenSKP 1.5.0: a concave four-sided face is no longer triangulated across its outside, classic textured materials are no longer all read as colorized, an empty SketchUp 2020 model opens, legacy dimensions keep their endpoints and placement, texts anchored to an entity read, guides keep their layer and hidden flag, and crafted files meet tighter bounds (archive entry sizes, object nesting, component hierarchies).
- A crafted SketchUp file with deeply nested legacy objects no longer crashes the program on Windows, whose main thread has a small stack: SketchUp files are read on a thread with a stack of their own.

## [0.10.0] - 2026-11-08

### Changed

- ConstructCAD is now ConstructCraft. The application, its window, installers, packages and documentation carry the new name, and the desktop program is `constructcraft` (the repository moves to `francisreboucas/constructcraft`). Nothing else changes: the `ccad` command line, the Lua `ccad` table, the MCP server, the `.ccad`, `.ccomp`, `.ccmacro` and `.ccgraph` files and the `ConstructCAD_*` IFC property sets keep their names, preferences and recovery copies stay where they were, and installing the new version replaces the old one (the Windows installer upgrades it in place, the Debian package replaces `constructcad`). Copies older than this one find the update but cannot install it themselves; download it from the release page once.
- Section marks in plans follow NBR 6492 (#197): a 12 mm head circle split by a line, with the drawing number over the sheet number (the last word of the section's name over a dash while it is on no sheet), on a filled triangle pointing where the section looks, and an extra-wide dash-dot line. A section line can be split, drawing only a piece next to each head (*Split line*, *Segment at start/end*). In plans the Select tool picks a mark (its properties show the section's), drags it to move the section and drags its handles to move an end or shorten a piece; Space flips it and Delete deletes the section. `view_set` takes `segments`.
- Ribbon tabs are as wide as their own titles instead of all as wide as the widest, and narrow windows shrink the room between them evenly (#187). In *Architecture → Model*, *Component* now comes after *Sweep*, next to the other component buttons.

### Added

- Lua scripts and plugins (#72, first part). `ccad lua script.lua` runs a Lua 5.4 script that calls the engine's commands with `ccad.run` and keeps their results in variables, which JSON scripts cannot do (`examples/small_house.lua`). A plugin is a folder with a `plugin.toml` manifest and Lua code that registers commands with `ccad.command`. Its commands join the catalog as `<plugin>__<command>`, so `ccad run`, scripts and MCP agents call them like the built-in ones; `ccad --plugins <folder>` loads them and `ccad plugins` lists them (`examples/plugins/quick_rooms`). Each plugin command is one undo step, and a failed one leaves nothing behind. Lua runs sandboxed: no files, no loading of code, an instruction and a memory limit. Plugins query and edit the model, and they need the `files` permission to import, export or save. The new error code `script` reports a failed script or plugin. Plugins are listed in the community index, [constructcad-plugins](https://github.com/francisreboucas/constructcad-plugins); the app's plugin manager comes next.
- Plugin manager (#72). *Manage → Plugins* opens the Plugins window. *Installed* lists the plugins of the plugins folder (set in *Preferences → File locations*, by default a folder in the app's data folder), with their commands and any error; it turns them on or off, allows file access for those that ask for it, and removes them. *Available* reads the community index and installs or updates a plugin after checking the download against the index's checksum, and only if the plugin loads. *Manage → Plugin Commands* lists the installed plugins' commands and opens one in the command palette, which now also finds plugin commands. Without the app, `ccad plugins install <file.zip> <folder>` and `ccad plugins remove <id> <folder>` do the same.

- Scripted components (#170). A component piece can be a Lua script that declares parameters (length, angle, number, yes/no, text, choice, material) and hotspots, and returns pieces from `geometry(p)`: boxes, cylinders, extrusions, profiles, revolutions, sweeps, blends and meshes (`examples/components/cabinet.lua`). Parameters without `instance = true` are shared by the type; with it, each element has its own. Scripts are pure and sandboxed, run once per set of values, and a failing one keeps its last pieces and reports the error with its line. New commands: `component_script_set`, `component_params_get`, `component_params_set` (types, or several elements at once, in one undo step) and `component_hotspot_move`. The properties palette edits every parameter kind and shows the shared ones and the script's errors; the component editor adds script pieces; hotspots are dragged as grips in plans. Material parameters count as uses of their material. Projects are saved as format 18.
- Terrain. *Massing & Site → Site → Terrain* sketches a boundary and places measured points with their elevations. The points are triangulated with the boundary (constrained Delaunay through the `spade` crate), the earth closes down to a flat base, plans draw contour lines and hatch the earth above the cut, and sections cut it in the new *Earth* pattern (a woven hatch). Properties edit the base depth and each point and show the elevations, area, volume and warnings; schedules list terrain. New commands: `topography_create`, `topography_points_set` and `topography_type_create`. IFC export writes an `IfcGeographicElement` (`TERRAIN`) that imports back as terrain. Projects are saved as format 19.
- A *Massing & Site* ribbon tab holds the massing tools, which leave the *Architecture* tab, and the terrain.
- Building pads. *Massing & Site → Site → Building Pad* sketches a flat pad at an elevation on a terrain, which is graded with cut and fill slopes out to the existing ground. Cut and fill volumes are exact between the two surfaces. Plans draw the pad with its elevation, slope ticks, the daylight line and the existing contours dashed; sections dash the existing ground. Properties set the elevation and each slope (the type's or the pad's own) and show the area, volumes and net; pad schedules total the volumes. Pads move and are deleted with their terrain. New commands: `pad_create` and `pad_type_create`. IFC export keeps them in the terrain's `ConstructCAD_Pads` property set, which import reads back. Projects are saved as format 20.
- Japanese interface (日本語, `ja`), contributed by Filippo Amoroso. It is picked from the system language for `ja` and `ja-JP`. Its characters come from the system's CJK fonts, like the Chinese interfaces.
- Curtain walls (#155). A curtain wall type holds vertical and horizontal grids (fixed distance, fixed number or maximum spacing, justified at the start, center or end), the default panel (glass, solid, empty, or a door, window or other component) and interior and border mullions. Each wall keeps its own lines, merged cells and swapped panels, on straight and arc walls (flat panels on the chords); a straight curtain wall inside a parallel wall opens it like a door. The wall tool's type list offers *+ Curtain Wall* when the project has none, *Type Properties* edit the grids, panel and mullions, and *Architecture → Build → Curtain Grid* adds and removes lines, merges and splits segments and swaps panels with clicks in plans, elevations and sections. Schedules have panel count, glazed area and mullion length. New commands `curtain_type_create`, `curtain_get`, `curtain_grid_set` and `curtain_panel_set`. IFC export writes an `IfcCurtainWall` with an `IfcPlate` per panel and an `IfcMember` per mullion, and the grid in a `ConstructCAD_Curtain` property set that import reads back; curtain walls from other programs are rebuilt from their parts when they make a plain straight grid.
- Visual programming (#191). *Manage → Macros → Visual Programming* (or `--graph` at startup) opens a node editor under the views. Every engine command is a node, with ports from its parameters, beside built-in nodes (inputs, math, logic, lists, geometry, element parameters) and pure Lua nodes; a list given where a node takes one value runs it once per item (shortest, longest or cross lacing). Running a graph again updates the elements it made instead of adding copies: each keeps its id, mark, comments and phases, new ones are made and those no longer made are deleted, all in one undo step. Graphs are `.ccgraph` files; `ccad graph <file> key=value` runs one without the app, and the commands `graph_run`, `graph_check`, `graph_nodes`, `graph_elements` and `graph_release` drive them (`examples/graphs/house.ccgraph`). Projects are saved as format 21.
- Title blocks are components (#192). Sheets draw an editable title block instead of the fixed one: border, block, logos and texts drawn with the sketch tools in the title block editor (*Modify → Title Block → Edit Title Block*, *View → Documentation → New Title Block*), measured in paper millimeters and adapting to any paper size. Texts fill in from fields: sheet number, count, scale and paper, project data and the project's own fields (*Project Information*), the date and the issue date, and per-sheet values. Sheets gain portrait orientation, custom paper sizes and an issue date. Title blocks travel in `.ccomp` files and the component library. The old block is now the built-in *Standard* title block, given to older files on load with captions in the interface language. New commands `sheet_set`, `sheet_fields`, `title_block_create` and `component_sheet_piece_add`/`set`/`delete`; `project_info_set` takes `fields`. Projects are saved as format 22.
- Level marks (#202). *Annotate → Levels → Level in Plan* writes, on a circle with two filled quarters, the finished level of the floor under the point and, below the shelf, the raw level of its structure (the top of the layers under its finish); off any floor it writes the plan's level. *Level in Section* writes a point's height on a triangle pointing down to it, with the raw level when the point lies on the top of a cut floor, and snaps to the section's lines. The values follow the model; a mark can set either level by hand, hide the raw one or put the values on the left, and its type holds the text size, the datum written for the project's zero and the suffixes. New commands `level_mark_create` and `level_mark_type_create`.
- Stair step numbers (#199). *Number steps* (in the properties palette and the stair tool's options bar) writes a number in every tread and winder of the stair in plans, from a *Start number*; the floor the stair arrives at takes the next one, and landings none. `stair_create` takes `numbers` and `number_start`.
- Typed lengths in sketches (#179). After the first point of a floor, ceiling, roof, mass, terrain, shaft or component sketch, typing places the next point: a line's length and, after Tab, its angle; a rectangle's width and depth; the radius of a circle, polygon or arc; the distance of a Move or Copy. Enter places the point and Backspace edits the value.
- Drafting views (#148). *View → Drafting View* makes a 2D view independent of the model, with its own scale, detail level and visibility, for standard details: it shows only what is drawn in it (texts, dimensions, imported drawings and pictures, detail lines, regions and detail components), and goes on sheets and exports like any view. `view_create` takes `kind: "drafting"`. The scale lists of all views gain 1:1, 1:2 and 1:5.
- Detail lines (#149). *Annotate → Detail Line* (`DL`) draws lines, polylines, arcs, circles, ellipses and rectangles that belong to one view (plans, ceiling plans, elevations, sections, legends, drafting views), in a built-in style (thin, medium, wide, hidden, dashed, center, dotted) or a project line style. They have grips and snapping, and *Trim/Extend*, *Split* and *Offset* work on them. DXF export puts them on a layer per style. New command `detail_line_create`.
- Line styles, filled regions and masking regions (#150). *Annotate → Line Styles* defines the project's line styles (name, weight, color, pattern); deleting a style in use moves its lines to another. *Annotate → Filled Region* sketches an outline, with openings, filled with a pattern in its color, and *Masking Region* one that hides the model under it in white, each with an optional boundary style and *Edit Boundary*. They draw over the model and under annotations, in exports too. New commands `line_styles_list`, `line_style_create`/`set`/`delete`, `filled_region_create`, `masking_region_create` and `filled_region_type_create`.
- Detail components (#151). 2D components made of lines, patterns, hatched shapes, texts, pictures and other detail components, for repeated construction details. *Annotate → Detail Component* (`DC`) places one with a rotation, scale and mirror, and *Create Detail Component* turns the selected detail lines, regions and texts into one. Import CAD's *Blocks as components* brings a DWG or DXF file's blocks in as detail components placed where the file inserts them, and DXF export writes them back as blocks. `.ccomp` files carry the components they nest. New commands `detail_component_create` and `detail_component_place`.
- Callouts and detail views (#152). *View → Callout* draws a rectangle in a plan, ceiling plan, section or elevation and makes a detail view of that region at a larger scale, live with the model and cropped to it, or points the callout to a drafting view instead. The mark shows the detail and sheet numbers; dragging it moves or resizes the region, a double-click opens the view, and deleting the view removes the mark. Plans, sections and elevations can be cropped (`view_set` `crop`). New commands `callout_create`, `callout_remove` and `callouts_list`.
- Explode imported drawings (#153). *Modify → Explode*, or *Explode* in an imported DWG or DXF drawing's properties, turns its visible layers into native elements of the view in one undo step: lines, arcs and circles into detail lines on a line style per layer (made in the layer's color when missing), fills into filled regions and texts into text notes; pictures stay. New command `cad_explode`.
- Site location (#209). *Manage → Project → Site Location* opens a map (OpenStreetMap) to search an address or place, or move the map under the marker, and sets the project origin's latitude, longitude, elevation (looked up from Open-Meteo), true north and address. It can bring in the surroundings from OpenStreetMap: streets with their names, paths, water, green areas and trees as a pinned imported drawing, and the buildings as context masses with their heights. New commands `site_get`, `site_set` and `site_context_import`. IFC export writes the site's location, elevation, address and true north, and import reads them back.
- The project browser and properties dock on either side of the window, stacked in one column or each in a column of its own (#210). Drag a panel's header onto a side of the window or onto the other panel, or right-click it for *Dock on the left*, *Dock on the right*, *Stack with…* and *Separate from…*. The layout is kept between sessions, and *Preferences → Interface* restores the default.
- A progress dialog while opening a project or importing IFC, DWG/DXF, SketchUp, pictures and component files (#211). The file is read in the background while the dialog shows its name, folder and size and the time elapsed. The project stays as it was until the file has been read, and *Cancel* stops waiting and changes nothing.
- Trackpad navigation (#174). Two fingers on a trackpad pan the views and a pinch zooms, while a mouse wheel still zooms around the cursor; on a Mac, Cmd and two fingers zoom too. This works in 2D and 3D views, the component, profile and title block editors, the visual programming editor and the site map. *Preferences → Interface → Navigation → Trackpad scrolling pans* turns it off, for a mouse that reports its wheel like a trackpad.
- Snaps for drafting (#159). Drawing tools snap to where two lines or arcs cross (walls, grids, outlines, imported drawings and detail lines), and from a curve's first point to the foot of the perpendicular on a line or arc and to the points where a line touches an arc or circle. The Detail Line tool takes typed values after the first point, as the sketches do: a line's length and, after Tab, its angle, a circle's or arc's radius, a rectangle's width and depth; Enter places the point.

### Fixed

- Walls drawn on a core or finish face left a square gap at L corners (#165). Corners now join where the drawn location lines meet.
- At a T-join, a layered wall's structure core now runs through the outer layers of the wall it meets to that wall's core, and plans draw the layers cleaned up there, with no line between cores of one material (#165).
- The wall tool's preview stayed centered on the cursor path whatever the *Location Line*, so a wall drawn on a face jumped across when finished (#198). The preview now shows the wall where it will be built.
- *Attach Top* did not pick its roof, ceiling or floor in 3D views; a click there only selected it.

- Stair shapes (#29): straight, straight with a landing, L-shaped (with a landing or 2 to 6 winders), U-shaped (with a gap between the flights) and spiral/helical (around an inner radius), turning left or right, with the risers of the first flight and the landing depth adjustable. Plans draw every tread, landing and winder (dashed above the cut) with the break line and the arrow along the walking line; IFC export writes the matching `IfcStair` type (`TWO_STRAIGHT_RUN_STAIR`, `QUARTER_TURN_STAIR`, `QUARTER_WINDING_STAIR`, `HALF_TURN_STAIR`, `SPIRAL_STAIR`) with one closed B-rep per piece.
- Stair construction on the type: solid (filled down to the floor, as before), cast in place (steps on an inclined waist slab of a given thickness, measured square to the pitch, and landing slabs; `WaistThickness` in IFC) or assembled (precast, steel or timber treads of a given thickness on side stringers or a central one, a central column on spirals, optional closed risers).
- Step rules on the type and Blondel's formula: minimum riser and tread and the range of `2 × riser + tread` (defaults 16 cm, 28 cm and 63–65 cm); the tread can be fixed or follow Blondel's formula from the riser. The properties palette shows risers, riser height, tread, run and Blondel's step with the broken rules, and the new `stair_check` command returns them; `element_get` lists them under `derived.check`.
- Railings that follow a stair (#29): *Add railings* in a stair's properties, or `stair_railings_add`, puts railings along its sides that climb at the pitch of the nosings, go round landings and winders and follow the stair when it changes, is copied, mirrored or deleted. Railing lengths are measured along their slope.
- Ramps (#29), a new category (*Ramp* in the Circulation group, `RP`): straight (flights in line with landings between them), L-shaped (around a corner landing), U-shaped (switchback flights joined by landings) or helical, turning left or right. The type sets the width, the slab thickness and the accessibility rules (steepest slope, highest rise per flight, shortest landing; by default 8.33 %, 0.80 m and 1.20 m): flights are laid out to meet them, and the properties palette and the new `ramp_check` command report the slope, the flights and the rules broken (too steep, a flight rising too high, a short landing). The instance may take a gentler or steeper slope and a longer landing. `ramp_create` and `ramp_type_create` are new; a project without a ramp type gets one with its first ramp. Plans draw the flights (dashed above the cut, with a break line), the arrow and the slope; schedules list width, slope, run and volume; IFC export writes `IfcRamp` (`STRAIGHT_RUN_RAMP`, `TWO_STRAIGHT_RUN_RAMP`, `QUARTER_TURN_RAMP`, `HALF_TURN_RAMP`, `SPIRAL_RAMP`). `stair_railings_add` (*Add railings*) also puts railings along ramps.
- Autosave and crash recovery (#51). A minute after a project gets unsaved changes (new, never-saved projects too), a copy of it is written in the background to the `recovery` folder of the app's data folder, only when the model changed since the last copy, through a temporary file and a rename. Saving, closing or quitting cleanly removes it. When ConstructCAD starts and finds copies left by a session that crashed or was killed, the *Recover Unsaved Work* dialog offers them: *Restore* opens the project with its unsaved changes (saving writes to its original file), *Discard* deletes the copy, *Cancel* keeps it for the next start.
- A panic in the interface writes a last recovery copy before the app closes, and every panic is appended to `crash.log` in the app's data folder (message, place and backtrace), since release builds on Windows have no console.
- Grids are named in series (#52). A new grid takes the name after the most recently placed grid parallel to it (within 10°): `1` → `2`, `09` → `10`, `A` → `B`, `Z` → `AA`, `A1` → `A2`, `1.1` → `1.2`. A grid across the existing ones starts a new series: letters across numbered grids, numbers otherwise. The name is unique among the grids (a taken one is skipped). The grid tool shows the name next to the cursor before the second click. `grid_create` uses these names when `name` is left out.
- Grid ends line up with the bubbles of the other grids (#52). When the grid tool places a point or an end handle of a selected grid is dragged, the end snaps (within the usual 10-pixel snap distance) to the line through the matching end of a parallel grid, perpendicular to it. The second point also keeps the grid parallel to that grid. A dashed guide from that grid's end shows the alignment. Model points and guide crossings still take priority.
- *Align Grid Ends* (*Architecture* and *Structure* tabs, *Datum* group; `grids_align_ends`): with one grid selected, every grid parallel to it (within 1°) takes its extents. Each end moves along its own line to the reference end on its side. Pinned grids are left alone. `ends` limits the move to the `start` (bubble) or `end` ends, and `grids` picks the grids to align.
- Modify tools in 3D views (#53): Move, Copy, Array, Rotate, Mirror (pick or draw the axis), Align and Join/Unjoin Geometry work in 3D views. Points are picked on a horizontal working plane at the elements' level (then at the height of the first point) or snap to model points near the pointer: element corners, column and component centers at their base and top, wall ends and grid crossings. The preview shows the elements' outline where they will go. Changes are plan transforms, so elements keep their levels. Trim/Extend, Split, Offset and Allow/Disallow Join need plan linework and say so in 3D views.
- Align takes columns and components, by the edge of their outline nearest to the point picked, in plans and in 3D. Alignment is in plan, so a column of an upper floor aligns onto the face of the column below. `element_align` takes `pick` and `reference_pick` for them (without `pick`, a column or component moves its insertion point onto the reference line, such as a grid).
- Dimension lines line up with other dimensions (#54). While the line of an aligned or linear dimension is placed, it snaps (within the usual 10-pixel snap distance) onto the line of a parallel dimension in the same view, shown by a dashed guide from that dimension's line.
- Continuous dimensioning (#54). The options bar of the aligned and linear dimension tools chooses *Single* or *Continuous*. In *Continuous*, after the first dimension each click on a point adds the next dimension of the chain, from the previous point, on the same dimension line and in the same direction; Enter, Esc or a right-click ends the chain. Each segment is its own dimension, whose ends follow the elements they were placed on. The new `dimension_chain_create` command places a whole chain (points in order, `kind`, `direction` or `angle`, `offset`) in one undo step.

### Changed

- Solid and cast-in-place stairs build each straight flight as one block from its side profile: the steps on top and, when cast in place, a continuous waist slab with a flat foot on the floor and a haunch under the landing it rises to, down to the landing's underside; the flight above starts from that underside. Sides show the steps and the soffit only, with no seams between treads.
- Spiral stairs turn no faster than leaves 2.20 m between a tread and the one a turn below (the tread on the walking line grows when the type's would), and a solid spiral is built as a helical slab instead of treads filled down to the floor; its curved sides and soffit read as smooth surfaces.

### Fixed

- The 3D view and picking in it could crash on a degenerate face (a footprint with no area, or points that are not finite numbers): its triangulation panicked inside earcutr. Such faces now triangulate in the plane of their points or give no triangles.
- A GPU error (a validation error or a failed allocation) no longer ends the app: it is logged. A 3D mesh larger than the GPU's buffer limit is not uploaded instead of failing validation.

### Added

- Steel and hollow sections: column types can be I/H, channel (U), angle (L), rectangular tube or round tube, and beam types I/H, U, L or tube, edited in the type dialog (section kind, size, web, flange or wall thickness, kept within the section). 3D, plans, legends and schedules draw and measure the real profile; IFC export uses the parametric IFC profiles (`IfcIShapeProfileDef`, `IfcUShapeProfileDef`, `IfcLShapeProfileDef`, `IfcRectangleHollowProfileDef`, `IfcCircleHollowProfileDef`). The template gains steel column and beam types and a steel material.
- *Join Slabs to Supports* (*Structure → Analysis*, `slab_join_supports`): joins floors with the beams, structural columns and structural walls whose bodies overlap them, so the shared volume counts once and sections show one piece.
- Analytical model: structural columns and beams as center lines between shared nodes — beams framed into columns end on the column axis, beams ending on beams end on their line, members split where others connect, column bases are supports. *Analytical Model* opens a 3D view of it over the model drawn faint (any 3D view can switch with its *Analytical Model* property; `view_create` kind `analytical`); `analytical_model` returns it as JSON and *Export Analytical Model* (`analytical_export`) saves it for structural analysis programs.
- Reinforcement, a new category: sets of bars in beams (top and bottom bars, stirrups) and columns (longitudinal bars around the section, ties), with their cover and bar type (5 to 16 mm in the template). Bars follow their host and go with it; *Structure → Reinforcement*: *Typical Reinforcement* for the selected (or all) beams and columns, a *Reinforcement View* (3D, bars over the faint model) and a *Steel Schedule* (host, diameter, bar count, bar length, total length and weight, with totals). Beam and column properties list their sets, editable and removable. IFC export writes `IfcReinforcingBar`s (swept disks along each bar) with their type objects. Engine commands `rebar_create` and `rebar_typical`; `element_get` reports count, lengths and weight.
- Bracing and trusses (*Structure → Framing*): *X Bracing* between two selected columns (`braces_between_columns`, or `brace_create` between any two points in space) and the *Truss* tool, which lays out Pratt, Howe or Warren trusses from two clicks with the height and panels of the options bar (`truss_create`): chords and diagonals are sloped beams, posts are columns, grouped. The analytical model connects their members at the panel points.
- Structural plans (*Structure → Documentation → Structural Plan*, `view_create` kind `structural_plan`): floor plans showing only the structure — slabs, structural walls and columns, beams (seen from below) and foundations — tagged with mark and section (`V1 15×40`, `P1 20×20`). *Annotate → Structural Tags* shows the same tags in any floor plan, and plans gain *Structural Plan* and *Structural Tags* properties. *Number Structure* (`structure_number`) numbers beams, columns and pad footings in reading order.

- Building services (MEP), a new *MEP* tab for plumbing and electrical work:
  - Pipes and conduits, two new categories: straight runs with an elevation at each end, either level, sloped or vertical (risers). The *Pipe* (PI) and *Conduit* (CN) tools chain runs, and a drain keeps falling from one run to the next. Their types set the outside diameter and material; the template has PVC supply pipes (20–32 mm), drain pipes (40–100 mm) and flexible conduits (20–32 mm).
  - Systems and circuits are project settings: cold and hot water, sanitary drainage, vent and rainwater, from the template, plus power and lighting circuits fed by a distribution board, with a voltage. Each has a code shown in tags and a color. *Systems and Circuits* edits them and shows the circuit loads.
  - Pipes take their system's defaults. Supply pipes run in the walls at 0.60 m, drains under the floor at -0.30 m falling 2 % up to 75 mm and 1 % above. Conduits run in the slab above.
  - Plumbing fixtures and electrical devices are components with MEP data. Fixtures carry drainage fixture units, a supply weight and hot water; devices are general-purpose and dedicated outlets, switches, ceiling and wall lights, and distribution boards, with their power in VA. The library gains a shower, kitchen sink, floor drain, outlets, switch, lights and board. *Plumbing Fixture* and *Electrical Device* place them, devices at their usual mounting height and on the chosen circuit.
  - Plumbing and electrical plans (*MEP → Documentation*, `view_create` kinds `plumbing_plan` and `electrical_plan`) draw pipes and conduits over the architecture in their system's color. Tags give the system code, diameter and slope (`ES Ø100  i = 1,0 %`), and conduit tags list their circuits. Devices use the usual plan symbols: an outlet triangle filled by height, a ceiling light circle with its circuit and power, the switch, the board. Ordinary plans leave pipes, conduits and devices out. Pipes and conduits show in 3D, sections and elevations.
  - Schedules: pipes and conduits list system, diameter, length and slope (*MEP Schedules*), and components gain *System*, *Power* and *Fixture units* fields.
  - IFC export writes `IfcPipeSegment` and `IfcCableCarrierSegment` (circular profiles along each run, with length quantities and type objects). Devices are written as `IfcOutlet`, `IfcSwitchingDevice`, `IfcLightFixture` and `IfcElectricDistributionBoard`. Systems become `IfcDistributionSystem` and circuits `IfcDistributionCircuit`, grouping their elements. CI validates an MEP fixture.
  - Networks and fittings: pipes of one system, and any conduits, connect where their ends meet (within a centimeter) or where an end lands on another run. Fittings are derived like wall joins. Elbows (with their angle), couplings, reducers, tees and crosses show in plumbing plans as dots in the system's color, and conduit junctions as boxes. They appear as sockets in 3D and are exported as `IfcPipeFitting`/`IfcCableCarrierFitting` with type objects, grouped in their system. `mep_fittings` and *Systems and Circuits* count them by kind, angle and diameter.
  - *Connect to Network* (`mep_connect`) branches the selected fixtures to the nearest sanitary drain, falling towards it with a drop from the floor and 100 mm for toilets, and to the nearest cold and hot water pipes. It joins devices to the nearest conduit with a drop from their mounting height, on their circuit. The pipe and conduit tools snap to run ends, runs and fixtures.
  - Sizing (*MEP → Analysis → Sizing*, `mep_sizing`), from the networks with simplified tables of common Brazilian practice:
    - Sanitary drains carry the fixture units upstream of them, towards the network's lowest node, and get the usual branch diameters, with at least 100 mm after a toilet.
    - Water pipes carry a flow of 0.3 √ΣP l/s from the weights of the fixtures they feed, coming from the highest node, and get the smallest PVC pipe kept at most 3 m/s.
    - Circuits get their design current, the next standard breaker and a copper conductor that carries it (at least 1.5 mm² for lighting, 2.5 mm² for power). The conductor grows until the voltage drop to the farthest device, measured along the conduits (else in a straight line), is at most 4 %.
    - *Apply Diameters* (`mep_size_apply`) gives undersized pipes the smallest pipe type that is wide enough.
  - Panel diagrams, a new view category (*MEP → Documentation → Panel Diagram*, `view_create` kind `panel_diagram`): the single-line diagram of a distribution board and its load schedule, with the circuits' points, load, current, breaker, conductor and voltage drop, the totals and the main breaker. Diagrams go on sheets like any drawing and are deleted with their board. Views draw their own headings in the interface language.
  - In plumbing and electrical plans, the architecture is drawn faint (a new faint pen, with fills lightened) so the services stand out.
  - Engine commands `pipe_create`, `conduit_create`, `systems_list`, `system_create`, `system_set`, `system_delete`, `system_assign` and `circuit_loads` (installed load and current per circuit and board). `component_place` takes a `system`. Systems resolve by id, name, code or kind.

- Component pieces can be profiles of lines and arcs drawn on the plan, front or side plane, extruded along the plane's normal or revolved about an axis in the plane, and any piece can be a void cut out of the solid pieces it overlaps. Plans, 3D, schedules, legends and IFC export use the resolved pieces; the component editor edits them and previews plan, front and 3D edges of the real faces.
- Interface languages: Ukrainian, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Polish, chosen in the language menu, by `--lang` or from the system locale. Every language except English uses a decimal comma.

### Changed

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-20

### Added

- Text notes (*Annotate → Text*, TT) in any 2D view: single-line text, or multiline text word-wrapped to a width in paper millimeters; rotation; text types with their size and default leader symbol. Leaders are drawn from the text with one segment or two (an elbow), ending in an arrow, open arrow, triangle, dot, circle, square, filled square, tick or nothing; *Add Leader* adds more to a selected text. Double-click a text to edit it; the properties edit its text, width, rotation and each leader's symbol. Engine commands `text_create`, `text_add_leader` and `text_remove_leader`; texts move, copy and rotate with the other elements and stay readable when mirrored.
- Engine (`cc_engine`): the operations of ConstructCAD as named commands that run without the GUI, each with typed JSON parameters and a generated JSON Schema — project files, undo/redo, project information, units and language; IFC import/export, sheet PDF/SVG, schedule CSV, component files; queries of categories, levels, types, elements (with derived length, height, thickness, volume, room area), views, materials and schedule tables; creation of walls, doors, windows, floors, ceilings, roofs, rooms, grids, columns, beams, stairs, railings and components with the drawing tools' defaults; move, copy/array, rotate, mirror, pin, flip, delete, wall joins, trim/extend, split, offset, align and end points; element, type, view and material parameters; types (duplicate, delete), components, materials, levels, views and sheets. Levels, types, views and materials are named by id or name; edits that would leave the model invalid are refused without an undo step.
- `ccad` command line (`cc_cli`, no GUI dependencies): list commands and schemas, run one command (JSON or `key=value` arguments) or a script, open or import a project and save it afterwards. Included in release packages.
- MCP server (`ccad mcp`): an in-house Model Context Protocol server over stdio exposing every engine command as a tool, so AI agents can build and edit models.
- Deleting a type that no element uses (`type_delete`); creating elevations and levels at a given elevation and name from commands.
- Dimensions as engine commands: `dimension_create` (associated with the elements or level lines its ends lie on) and `dimension_set_value` (drives what it measures); `elements_turn` (the Space key), `elements_export` and `elements_paste` (element sets between sessions).
- Documentation of the engine, the command line, scripts, macros and MCP (`docs/automation.md`, `docs/automacao.md`), a command reference generated from the catalog (`docs/commands.md`, `ccad reference`; a test keeps it current) and an example script (`examples/small_house.ccmacro`).
- Doors and windows can be placed by a point near a wall, without its id; sheets can be referred to by number.
- Macros (*Manage → Macros*): record the changes you make as a script of engine commands (`.ccmacro`, dragged values recorded once, cancelled dialogs left out) and play macros back; `ccad script` runs the same files.
- Join and unjoin geometry (*Modify → Geometry*, in plans and sections; engine commands `geometry_join` and `geometry_unjoin`): where two joined elements overlap, one cuts the other — structural columns and beams cut walls, floors, ceilings and roofs; floors, ceilings and roofs cut walls; walls cut architectural columns. The shared volume is counted once (quantities, schedules, IFC), the cut body loses it, and plans and sections draw two elements cut in the same material as one piece, without the line between them. Joins undo, survive saving and go with deleted elements; `element_get` lists `joined_with`. Columns gain a *Structural* property (on by default).
- *Select All Instances* (*Modify → Selection*, SA): selects every element of the selected elements' types shown in the active view; `elements_list` filters by `type` and by `view`.
- Groups (*Modify → Group*: Group GP, Ungroup UG; engine commands `group_create`, `group_ungroup`, `groups_list`): picking one member selects the whole group, so its elements move, copy and rotate together; an element belongs to one group at most, groups undo and lose deleted members, and `element_get` shows `group`.
- *Measure* (*Modify → Measure*, ME): measure between two references in any 2D view — the clicks snap to the view's linework; the status bar shows the distance, ΔX, ΔY and angle, and the distance between two parallel lines picked; nothing is created (engine query `measure`).
- Ribbon and quick access buttons are interface commands: each knows its label, shortcut, description, the engine command it runs and when it is available, so buttons enable themselves and their tooltips show the shortcut, what the command does, why it is unavailable (no selection, nothing to undo, no sheet open…) and the engine command scripts use for the same change. Two-letter shortcuts come from the same table and say why when they cannot run.
- Two-tone interface icons: strokes in the ink tone and soft fills in the theme's tint, with badges for commands that create (new views, components), import, export or remove. Theme colors are tokens with a dark set alongside the light one, ready for a dark theme.
- The ribbon collapses to its tabs (Ctrl+F1, a double click on a tab or the arrow at the end of the tabs) and the choice is remembered; while collapsed, a click on a tab shows its panel over the views until a command runs. When the window is narrower than a tab's groups, they scroll sideways with arrows at the ends.
- Command palette (Ctrl+Shift+P, or the magnifier after the ribbon tabs): finds interface commands, views to open, plans and schedules to create, and engine commands by name, description or shortcut (accents and case aside); engine commands open a form with their JSON arguments and run in the open project.
- Accessibility: the custom ribbon, palette, browser, home screen, dialog and property widgets report their role, name and state to screen readers. Interface tests drive the ribbon and the palette headlessly (egui_kittest), and screenshot tests of every ribbon tab, the narrow and collapsed ribbon, a rich tooltip, the palette and an icon gallery in both themes run on request.
- Interface guide (`docs/interface.md`, `docs/interface.pt-BR.md`): ribbon, tooltips, badges, collapsing, the command palette and its engine forms, keyboard, accessibility, and for developers how to add interface commands and icons and run the interface tests.

### Changed

- Cut hatching belongs to its element, so it highlights with it and joined geometry can trim it; the 3D boolean engine moved from `cc_ifc` to `cc_geom` (`cc_geom::csg`).
- Ribbon: *Load component* and *New component* use the component icon with an import or new badge, the sheet exports carry an export badge, and new views a new badge; a two-letter shortcut that cannot run says why in the status bar instead of doing nothing.
- `.ccad` format version 6 (joined geometry, structural columns, groups; version 5 added text notes); older files still open.
- The desktop app runs on the engine: its drawing tools, modify tools, properties, dialogs, clipboard, dimensions, document actions and undo/redo change the model through the same commands as the command line and MCP. Edits that would leave the model invalid are refused with a message; dragged values remain one undo step.
- Deleting a level also unconnects columns and stairs whose top was attached to it.
- IFC import without dropping classes: every physical object of an IFC2X3/IFC4 file comes in. Products become native elements whenever a native element holds their geometry exactly; everything else (stairs, railings, members, plates, curtain walls, MEP segments and fittings, terrain, curved walls, classes from other schemas) becomes a generic component that keeps its IFC class, predefined type, object type and exact geometry, and is exported back as the same class (with its IFC4 type object, or as a named `IfcBuildingElementProxy` for classes IFC4 lacks). `IfcGrid` axes become grid lines.
- In-house IFC geometry engine: placements (local and grid), polylines, indexed curves with arcs, circles, ellipses, trimmed and composite curves, B-splines (rational too); every parametric profile, arbitrary profiles with voids, center-line, composite, derived and mirrored profiles; extrusions, revolutions, swept disks and directrix sweeps; CSG primitives; boolean union, difference and intersection; half-space clipping; faceted and advanced B-reps; face sets and mapped items. Plane angle, area and volume units are read.
- Openings (`IfcRelVoidsElement`) are cut from imported generic objects with in-house mesh booleans; slabs, coverings, roofs, columns and beams with openings come in as generic objects so their holes are kept.
- Property and quantity sets of imported generic objects (their own and their type's) are kept with their IFC value types, converted to SI units, listed in Properties and written back on IFC export.
- Components can hold fixed mesh pieces; plans draw them cut at the cut plane with the edges seen from above.
- Properties show the IFC class, predefined type and object type of imported objects, and the import message counts the objects brought in as generic objects.
- `export_ifc` example accepts an `.ifc` file (import and export again); CI validates a hand-written IFC4 fixture covering many classes with IfcOpenShell.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-19

### Added

- Modify tools: Move (MV), Copy (CO, multiple), Rotate (RO, with center and typed angle), Mirror by picking an axis (MM) or drawing it (DM), linear Array (AR), Trim/Extend to Corner (TR) or to a reference (TX), Split (SL), Offset (OF), Align (AL), allow/disallow joins at wall ends, and Pin/Unpin (PN/UP); previews, snapping and one undo step per edit. Walls take their doors and windows along; mirrored openings swap their hinge side.
- Clipboard (*Modify → Clipboard*): Cut (Ctrl+X), Copy (Ctrl+C) and Paste (Ctrl+V) of elements within the project, placing the copy with a click in a plan or aligned to the same place or to the current level; walls bring their doors and windows, and a cut can be pasted after its originals are gone. Match Type Properties (MA) picks an element and gives its type to others of the same category.
- Typed length and angle while drawing walls and moving, copying or arraying: digits go to the length, Tab switches to the angle (from the previous wall or the X axis, on the cursor's side), Enter applies; lengths are read in the project's length unit.
- Temporary dimensions on selection: a single selected wall, beam or grid shows its distance to the nearest parallel wall, grid or column on each side, columns and components along X and Y, doors and windows along their wall; click a value and type to move the element.
- Associative dimensions: in plans each end of a new dimension follows the element it was placed on (walls, grids, columns, beams, doors, windows, components), through moves, rotations, stretches and undo; angular dimensions also keep their vertex at the intersection of the measured lines; in elevations and sections ends follow elements (with their height from the element's base or wall top) and level lines, and editing a dimension between levels raises or lowers the upper level; the dimension line stays where it was placed. Copies follow the copied elements; deleting an element frees the end. The sample project's dimensions follow its grids.
- Dimension values are editable: click the text of a selected dimension, double-click a dimension or type in Properties; in plans the new value moves what the dimension touches at its moving end (crossing elements translate, walls/beams/grids along it stretch), in one undo step. Angular values rotate; radial and diameter values are read-only.
- Dimension kinds: linear (horizontal or vertical by where the line is placed), angular (between two picked lines), radial and diameter (picked on any arc or circle in the view), besides aligned; one shared layout draws them in views, sheets and tool previews. Project files move to format version 4 (older files load with aligned dimensions).
- Selecting objects with the Select tool (in any view) opens the Modify tab.
- Zoom menu on the view, sheet and 3D zoom button: zoom in region (drag a window), zoom out (2x), zoom to fit, zoom all to fit, zoom sheet size (paper millimeters at screen size) and previous/next pan/zoom; the last command chosen becomes the button's action. In 3D views the window zoom frames the dragged region and the history keeps camera positions.
- Interface icons redrawn as SVG line drawings (`assets/icons/ui`), rasterized with resvg at the exact pixel size and aligned to the pixel grid, so they stay crisp at any scale.
- Ribbon: long labels wrap onto two lines, and panels with many tools stack small icon buttons with tooltips.
- Material browser (*Manage → Materials*): searchable list with swatches and four tabs — identity (description, class, keywords, manufacturer, model, cost, URL, keynote, mark), graphics (shading color, transparency, surface and cut patterns), appearance (render color, finish) and physical properties (thermal and mechanical); new, duplicate, rename (updating every type) and delete (refused while in use).
- Material cut patterns in plans (per wall layer, columns) and sections, at a constant paper size; transparent materials blended in 3D, elevations and sections, and exported to IFC surface styles.
- Project units (*Manage → Project Units*): unit, decimals and symbol for lengths, areas, volumes, angles and slopes, a currency symbol and the decimal symbol / digit grouping, applied to dimensions, tags, schedules, properties and length fields.
- Navigation cube in 3D views (faces, edges and corners, compass ring, home view), temporary dimensions with angles while drawing, and alignment guides from acquired points.
- Space rotates selected components and columns by 90° and flips selected doors and windows; the quick access toolbar gains Export PDF, Aligned Dimension, Default 3D View and Section.
- Parametric components: definitions made of boxes, cylinders and extruded profiles sized by expressions over named type or instance parameters; a component editor with live expression checking and plan/front previews; a starter library (table, chair, bed, sofa, toilet, washbasin, wardrobe); the *Component* tool (CM) with rotation; instance parameter overrides in Properties; components in 3D, plans, elevations, sections, legends and schedules; `.ccomp` files to save and load components; IFC export and import as `IfcFurniture`, `IfcSanitaryTerminal`, `IfcLightFixture` and `IfcBuildingElementProxy`, keeping parametric types and instance values on a round trip.
- IFC import (*File → Import IFC…*): an in-house STEP parser and a mapping of IFC2X3/IFC4 storeys, walls, doors, windows, slabs, ceilings, roofs, spaces, columns and beams (extruded solids, B-reps and face sets; millimeter and imperial units) to native elements and types; export → import round trips are tested.
- Sheet export to vector PDF and SVG (*File → Export PDF/SVG…*); the sheet is composed once in `cc_views`, so the screen and the files match.
- Configurable schedules: choose and order fields (instance, type and computed values), sort ascending or descending, filter by text, show or hide totals, and export to CSV (semicolon-separated when the decimal comma is used).
- Gable and hip roofs computed as the lowest of the planes rising from each sloped eave, with ridge and hip lines in plan and faceted B-rep IFC export.
- Columns (rectangular/round sections, rotation, top constraint), beams, straight stairs and railings, with types, plan symbols (cut columns, dashed beams, stair treads with break line and arrow), legends, schedules and IFC export; new *Structure* tab and CL/BM/ST/RA shortcuts.
- Rooms: boundary detected from room-bounding walls around a point (openings do not break it), with number, name, height, area, perimeter and volume; room tags in plans, room schedule and `IfcSpace` export with base quantities.
- Grids: named grid lines with bubbles shown in every plan; elevation markers stay outside them.
- Aligned dimensions in any 2D view, on a new *Annotate* tab, snapping to the view's linework.
- Automatic door and window tags (marks) in floor plans.
- Room, grid and dimension types; projects created earlier gain default types when opened.
- Wall constraints: top constrained to a level with offset (follows the level), location line (centerline, core or finish faces), room bounding and structural parameters; the wall tool sets top constraint and location line in the options bar.
- Type parameters: wall function, layer functions (core and finishes), type mark, description, manufacturer, model, URL, keynote, classification code, fire rating, comments and cost.
- New Type Properties dialog with type switching, Duplicate, Rename, sectioned parameters and a layer editor.
- Persistent unique ids for the project, types and elements, used as IFC GlobalIds; walls export `Pset_WallCommon` and types `Pset_ManufacturerTypeInformation`.
- Close Project (*File* tab, Ctrl+W) returns to the home screen.
- Home screen with recent projects (with 3D thumbnails) and bundled samples, shown at startup when no project is given and from the Home button.
- Elevation markers in floor plans.
- IFC4 export (`cc_ifc` crate and *File → Export IFC…*), validated in CI with IfcOpenShell.
- Initial MVP: parametric walls, doors, windows, floors, ceilings and roofs; plans, ceiling plans, elevations, sections, 3D views, legends, schedules and sheets; English and Portuguese (Brazil) interface; `.ccad` project files.

### Changed

- `.ccad` format version 3 (components); older files still open and receive the component library.
- The sample house is furnished with library components.
- Robustness rules: clippy denies `unwrap`, `expect` and `panic!` outside tests and the workspace forbids `unsafe`; opening, saving and exporting are guarded so an internal error never loses the open project.
- Projects are repaired when opened: invalid numbers, missing levels or types, orphan doors and windows, broken view references are removed or reset, and the number of repairs is reported.
- Standard dialog layout for every window (header with close button, footer with right-aligned actions, highlighted main and red destructive buttons, alert icons); data dialogs have OK / Cancel and Cancel undoes their edits; deleting a level asks for confirmation.
- `.ccad` format version 2 (adds the fields above; version 1 files still open).
- Redesigned About dialog with version, author, license, repository and credits.
- Polished interface: light neutral theme with the Inter typeface, ribbon with icons grouped into captioned panels and a quick access toolbar, project browser with category icons and counts as an indented tree, property palette with headers, floating scale and zoom-to-fit control in 2D views and a clearer 3D orientation gizmo.

### Fixed

- A crafted file with cyclic hosting (e.g. a door hosting itself) crashed the application when the element was deleted.
